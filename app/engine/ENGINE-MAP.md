Bob's own code map lists these patches too: one line each, with the files they change, the lines they add and take out, and the main names they add.

## Rebase checklist, most fragile first

1. **The graph's expert and FFN builders** (`src/llama-graph.cpp`: `build_moe_ffn`, `build_ffn`, `build_attn_mha`). 0001 and 0003 put their mask and dials right where the experts are picked and their outputs summed. Upstream changes these often (new expert-routing modes, fused kernels, new archs). If a hunk moved, check that the mask still comes **before** the top-k pick (`ffn_moe_logits_masked`), and that the dials still multiply the expert weights and outputs (`ffn_moe_weights_dialed`, `ffn_moe_out_dialed`, `ffn_out_dialed`). A wrong placement still builds, but gives wrong answers.
2. **The server** (`tools/server/server-context.cpp`, `server-task.*`, `server.cpp`, `server-schema.cpp`). About 580 of our lines live in files upstream rewrites often (routes, task types, slot state, result JSON). Check that `/expert-mask` and `/dials` are still registered in `init_routes()` / `server_register_routes`, that the new task types are handled in the task switch, and that `expert_trace` still comes back in all three result shapes (plain, OpenAI chat, OpenAI chat stream).
3. **Layer counting with MTP layers.** Since b12282, `n_layer` = all layers minus the next-token (MTP) layers. The mask and dials take one row per layer that runs. Bob sends `engineLayers` rows (from `src/lab-gguf.js`); the Riker 41/40 bug was this. If upstream changes how `n_layer_nextn` counts, re-check `llama_model_n_layer` against Bob's `brainFacts().engineLayers`.
4. **The card-sharing protocol version** (`ggml-rpc.cpp`, `RPC_PROTO_*`). b12359 (llama.cpp 1.0) is version 10, b12282 was 9 and b12032 was 8; from Brain Engine 8, patch 0014 keeps protocol 9 numbering on the wire so builds 7 and up share (engine/README.md). Brain Engine 6 and older can't share with 7 and up. If the version changed, say so in the release note. Also check that 0004's `RPC_CMD_SET_TENSOR_HASH` path and `serialize_set_tensor` still match upstream's message layout.
5. **SCUT and RDMA sharing the HELLO caps field** (`ggml/src/ggml-rpc/transport.cpp`: `get_caps` / `update_caps`). 0005 claims one caps bit. If upstream adds caps (RDMA or anything new), make sure our bit doesn't collide, and that a peer without SCUT still connects on the plain link.

Then run `engine/try-newer.sh <tag>`. Every patch must go on, and the Linux build must pass. Then do the PC test: the Lab's Peek (trace), switching experts off (mask), the fNRI dials, and Bigger Bobs with and without SCUT.

## The patches

### 0001-expert-mask: switch experts off without reloading
- **Upstream files:** `include/llama.h`, `src/llama-adapter.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-context.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-model.cpp`, `tools/server/server-context.{h,cpp}`, `server-task.h`, `server.cpp`.
- **Hooks:**
  - A new adapter `llama_adapter_emask`, next to the control vector (`llama_adapter_cvec`), holds one keep-row per layer.
  - `llm_graph_context::build_moe_ffn` adds -inf to the router logits of switched-off experts **before** the top-k pick, so they're never chosen (`ffn_moe_logits_masked`).
  - `llama_context` holds the mask and rebuilds the graph params when it changes.
- **New API:** `llama_model_n_expert()`, `llama_model_n_expert_used()`, `llama_set_expert_mask(ctx, keep, len)`.
- **Server:** `GET /expert-mask` and `POST /expert-mask` with `{"keep": null | [ids] | [[ids per layer]]}`. It runs as a server task, so it waits for running slots.
- **Bob callers:** `src/lab.js` (Cut, Try it without), `src/lab-watch.js` (fNRI).
- **Re-check on rebase:** checklist items 1, 2 and 3. Also check that `llama_set_adapter_cvec` still sits where 0001 and 0003 add their setters next to it.
- **Tests:** `test/run-lab-test.js` and `test/run-lab-watch-test.js` check what Bob sends. The PC checks against a real engine: switch some experts off in the Lab and the answer changes, with no reload in the log.

### 0002-expert-trace: report which experts (or neurons) fired
- **Upstream files:** `include/llama.h`, `src/llama-context.{h,cpp}`, `tools/server/server-context.cpp` (most of it), `server-schema.cpp`, `server-task.{h,cpp}`.
- **Hooks:**
  - `llama_set_eval_callback()` sets or clears the graph eval callback on a live context. It waits for pending work first.
  - The server's slot gets a trace collector, `server_expert_trace`. It reads the `ffn_moe_topk` tensor per layer for MoE brains, or groups of FFN neurons for dense ones (`"kind": "neurons"`).
  - Results are added to the final completion JSON.
- **New API:** `llama_set_eval_callback(ctx, cb, user_data)`.
- **Server:** request fields `expert_trace: true` and `expert_trace_heads`. The reply's `expert_trace` holds `kind`, `n_layer`, `n_expert`, `n_expert_used`, `n_ff`, `n_prompt`, `rows` and `last` (the final token written, which no step read). It is in all three result shapes.
- **Bob callers:** `src/lab-peek.js` (Peek inside), `src/lab-watch.js` (fNRI), `src/llamacpp.js`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** checklist item 2. Also check the tensor name `ffn_moe_topk` (if upstream renames it, the trace comes back empty, not broken). With a trace on, the graph runs step by step and is slower; that is expected.
- **Tests:** `test/run-lab-watch-test.js` (the trace shape Bob reads). PC: Peek inside on Riker shows its 40 layers lighting up.

### 0003-dials: turn a part up or down without reloading
- **Upstream files:** the same set as 0001, plus `build_ffn` and `build_attn_mha` in `src/llama-graph.cpp`.
- **Hooks:**
  - A dials adapter next to the expert mask.
  - The graph multiplies:
    - one expert's weight and output (`ffn_moe_weights_dialed`, `ffn_moe_out_dialed`);
    - a dense FFN's output (`ffn_out_dialed`);
    - one attention head;
    - a whole attention or FFN block.
  - 1 = as built, 0 = silent.
- **New API:** `llama_set_dials(ctx, dials, n)` with `llama_dial` {kind, layer, index, scale} and `llama_dial_kind`.
- **Server:** `GET /dials`, and `POST /dials` with `{"dials": [{"kind": "expert" | "head" | "attn" | "ffn", "layer", "index", "scale": 0..8}]}`. `{"dials": null}` or `[]` puts everything back to 1.
- **Bob callers:** `src/lab-watch.js` (fNRI's dials).
- **Re-check on rebase:** checklist items 1 and 3. If `build_attn_mha`'s head layout changes (head-major vs token-major), the head dial scales the wrong slice and doesn't fail loudly.
- **Tests:** `test/run-lab-watch-test.js`. PC: turning an expert's dial to 0 matches switching it off with the mask.

### 0004-rpc-wifi: card sharing re-sends almost nothing
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/ggml-rpc.cpp`, `tools/rpc/rpc-server.cpp`.
- **Hooks:**
  - `serialize_set_tensor` / the set-tensor path offers a weight's hash first (`RPC_CMD_SET_TENSOR_HASH`) above a lower size threshold (`GGML_RPC_HASH_MIN`). A lender with `-c` (cache) that already has the weight skips the copy.
  - `ggml-rpc-server --seed FILE.gguf` fills the lender's cache from a brain file on that machine, then exits, so even the first load sends little.
- **Server flags and environment:** `rpc-server --seed`, `GGML_RPC_HASH_MIN`.
- **Bob callers:** `src/share-card.js` (starts `ggml-rpc-server -c` behind the passcode gate), `src/llama-tools.js`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** checklist item 4. If upstream changes the cache file layout, a lender's old cache is ignored (slower, not wrong).
- **Tests:** `test/run-share-brains-test.js` and `test/run-llama-tools-test.js` (how Bob starts the lender). There's no automatic check of the resend path against a real build yet; the PC check is a second load over Bob-Net in seconds, not minutes.

### 0005-scut: Bob's own fast link over Wi-Fi (experimental)
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/CMakeLists.txt` (adds `scut.cpp`), new `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/scut.{h,cpp}`, `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/transport.cpp`.
- **Hooks:**
  - `transport.cpp`'s `get_caps` / `update_caps` offer SCUT in the RPC HELLO.
  - `send_data` and the receive path switch to SCUT's paced framing when both ends agreed.
  - `socket_t::accept()` hands off to it.
  - `scut.h` explains the framing.
- **Environment:** `GGML_RPC_SCUT=1` on both ends (off by default). Also `GGML_RPC_SCUT_MBPS`, `GGML_RPC_SCUT_AWAKE`, `GGML_RPC_SCUT_PUBLIC_PORT`, and the test-only `GGML_RPC_SCUT_TEST_DROP` / `GGML_RPC_SCUT_TEST_UDP_PORT`. `GGML_RPC_DEBUG` adds logging.
- **Bob callers:** `src/share-card.js` (`scutEnv`), `src/share-brains.js`, `src/llamacpp.js`, `src/brain.js`, `src/firewall.js`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** checklist item 5. A peer without SCUT must still connect on the plain link, and answers must stay identical with SCUT on or off.
- **Tests:** `test/run-llama-tools-test.js` (env and workflow wiring). The PC/Go real test is Bigger Bobs over Wi-Fi with SCUT on and off: same answer, faster with it on.

### 0006-scut-seal: every SCUT packet signed and scrambled
- **Upstream files:** new `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/scut-seal.h` (header-only ChaCha20-Poly1305 from RFC 8439, plus HChaCha20; no OpenSSL), `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/scut.cpp`, `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/scut.h`. Sits on top of 0005, so it only touches SCUT's own files.
- **Hooks:**
  - The SCUT offer gets version 2 with the `OF_SEAL` flag. Engines without 0006 only take version 1, so they never switch to a link they can't read and stay on the plain link.
  - Each direction gets its own key, made with HChaCha20 from Bob's key and both offers.
  - Every datagram is the readable 20-byte header, an 8-byte counter (the nonce), the scrambled body and a 16-byte tag. The receive path checks the seal (`unseal`) before anything in the datagram is used; forged, damaged, wrong-key and replayed packets are dropped.
- **Environment:** `GGML_RPC_SCUT_KEY` (64 hex digits, the lending side, or one key for all), `GGML_RPC_SCUT_KEYS="host:port=hex,host=hex"` (the borrowing side, one per lender; `=off` means no SCUT with that one). Also `GGML_RPC_SCUT_FEC`.
- **Bob callers:** `src/scut-seal.js` (link keys, kept locked with `src/secret-box.js`), `src/bob-net-pair.js` (a key made at pairing), `src/share-card.js` and `src/share-brains.js` (pass the keys), `src/tools.js` (the key file is on the never-touch list).
- **Re-check on rebase:** 0005 must go on first. A sealed Bob and an unsealed one must fall back to the plain link, never a link one side can't read. Same answers sealed or not.
- **Tests:** `test/run-scut-seal-test.js` (keys, pairing, locked key file). The PC test is Bigger Bobs over Wi-Fi with SCUT on, sealed (the default) and unsealed.

### 0007-expert-trace-stream: the trace while the reply is written

- `tools/server/server-task.h`: `expert_trace_stream` in the request params; `expert_trace_part` (json, null unless asked) on a streamed partial result.
- `tools/server/server-schema.cpp`: the `expert_trace_stream` field.
- `tools/server/server-context.cpp`: `server_expert_trace` remembers how far the pieces sent so far reach (`sent_tag`, `sent_out`, `sent_rows`, reset in `clear()`). `send_partial_response()` fills `expert_trace_part` from the new `expert_trace_part(slot)`, which builds the rows since the last piece the same way `expert_trace_end()` builds all of them, and waits for the next piece when a layer is behind. Running counts (`seen_tag`, `seen_out`, `seen_prompt`) keep each piece's cost the same at any context length, and a piece carries at most `part_max_rows` (64) rows: a long prompt arrives as one piece, so only its end is sent and `from` says where it starts (the full trace at the end is unchanged). Only on top of 0002.
- `tools/server/server-task.cpp`: the piece goes into the plain and the chat-stream JSON of a partial result.

### 0008-scut-live: the link log, and SCUT that retunes itself
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/ggml-rpc.cpp`, `transport.{h,cpp}`, and SCUT's own `scut.{h,cpp}`.
- **Hooks:**
  - `rpc_dispatcher::work()` times every command, writes the `[link]` lines every `GGML_RPC_STATS` seconds, and wakes from its queue wait (`message_queue::pop_for`) to write the last one when commands stop.
  - `rpc_serve_client()` does the same on the lending side (a guard object, so the last lines are written however the loop ends); `send_msg` and `rpc_server::sync_all_backends` add up sending and card time.
  - `socket_t` gains `bytes_sent`, `bytes_received`, `link_name` and `link_note`; `impl::get_caps` / `update_caps` keep the reason SCUT isn't in use (`scut_why()`).
  - `scut.cpp`: counters and `note()`; receive reports (`T_RXR`, offer flag `OF_TUNE`), `on_report()` (the tuning), `flush()` sends a repair per `fec_k` packets, `judge()` only slows for lateness when the line was being filled.
- **Environment:** `GGML_RPC_STATS` (seconds, default 10, 0 = off), `GGML_RPC_SCUT_TUNE` (default 1).
- **Bob callers:** `src/link-log.js` (keeps the lines in `bob-net-link.log` and the logbook), `src/share-card.js` (`linkEnv`, the lender's output), `src/llamacpp.js` (the borrower's output), `src/server.js` (`/api/share-brains/link-log`), `public/share-brains.js` (Watch the link live, Link log).
- **Re-check on rebase:** 0005 and 0006 first. If upstream changes `rpc_dispatcher::work()` or the `rpc_serve_client` loop, the timing hooks move with them; the probe uses `RPC_CMD_GET_ALIGNMENT` for device 0, which must stay a command the server answers without touching the card.
- **Tests:** `test/run-link-log-test.js`. SCUT alone can be built without llama.cpp (scut.cpp, scut.h, scut-seal.h and a four-line stand-in for log.h) and run through a pretend Wi-Fi; do that after any change to the tuning. The PC test is Bigger Bobs over Wi-Fi with the Link log open on both Bobs.

### 0009-expert-mass: how much each expert really wrote (REAP)
- **Upstream files:** `tools/server/server-context.cpp`, `tools/server/server-task.h`, `tools/server/server-schema.cpp`. Server only; no ggml or card backend change. Sits on top of 0002's expert trace.
- **Hooks:**
  - New request field `expert_trace_mass` (with `expert_trace`). The trace's eval callback also reads each layer's `ffn_moe_weighted-<layer>` node (w · f(x)) and adds up the L2 norm of every picked expert's row.
  - Every expert trace now carries `experts` and `layers` (picks per expert per layer); with mass on, `mass` laid out the same. That is the Forge's counts file shape, so `pod/keep.py` reads it as it is.
  - A brain that weighs the experts' input instead of their output (llama4) gets `"mass": null` and `mass_why`.
- **Bob callers:** none in the app yet. The Forge's `pod/reap.py` and `pod/keep.py` (`--by counts`, `mass` or `reap`) use the same numbers on a rented card; this lets a home scan make them too.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the node name `ffn_moe_weighted` in `llama-graph.cpp` (`build_moe_ffn`), and that w there is the weight after the brain's own renormalising. Requests that don't ask must be unchanged.
- **Tests:** none automatic (see below). The PC check is one `expert_trace_mass` request on Riker: `mass` has one list per layer, and every expert with picks has mass above 0.

### 0010-expert-trace-once: the trace read once per word

- **Upstream files:** `include/llama.h`, `src/llama-context.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-cparams.h`, `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}`, `tools/server/server-context.cpp`.
- **Hooks:**
  - `llama_cparams::keep_expert_ids`, set by `llama_set_keep_expert_ids()` (marks the scheduler for a new memory layout, `sched_need_reserve`); `llm_graph_params::allow_reuse` compares it, so the graph is rebuilt when it changes.
  - `llm_graph_context::build_moe_ffn()`: with it on, `ggml_set_output()` on the node `ffn_moe_topk` views, so no later layer is given its memory.
  - `llama_get_tensors()`: queues every copy with `ggml_backend_tensor_get_async()` on the tensor's own backend, then one `ggml_backend_synchronize()` per backend used.
  - `server_expert_trace::cb_eval()`: while asked, it remembers every `ffn_moe_topk-<layer>` it passes (`pending`) and only asks to stop at the last layer with experts (`last_moe_il`, learned on each request's first step); there it reads all of them with `llama_get_tensors()`. `expert_trace_begin()` turns `keep` on for expert traces only; the end of a trace turns it off.
- **Bob callers:** none new; the same `expert_trace` requests (`src/llamacpp.js`) get it.
- **Only on top of 0002, 0007 and 0009** (with `expert_trace_mass` the trace keeps the old way, since the mass reads every layer's outputs anyway).

### 0011-rpc-read-ahead: one trip a word instead of several
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/ggml-rpc.cpp` only (the client half).
- **Hooks:**
  - `rpc_dispatcher::graph_compute()` calls `ahead_learn()` before it sends a graph (the reads since the last graph are what follows that graph) and `ahead_ask()` after (one message carrying every read foreseen for this graph).
  - `rpc_dispatcher::get_tensor()` is what `ggml_backend_rpc_buffer_get_tensor` and `ggml_backend_rpc_get_tensor_async` now call: it takes the answer from what was asked ahead when there is one, and sends the read as before when there isn't.
  - `rpc_dispatcher::work()`: a message with `ahead` items sends every `GET_TENSOR` request first, then reads the answers in order.
  - `send()` / `send_async()` call `ahead_forget()` for anything that isn't a read or a graph.
- **Environment:** `GGML_RPC_AHEAD` (default 1, 0 = off).
- **Bob callers:** none; `src/link-log.js` keeps the `[link]   read ahead: ...` line like any other.
- **Re-check on rebase:** it leans on graphs keeping their `uid` from one word to the next (that is what makes upstream send `GRAPH_RECOMPUTE`), and on the card server answering commands in the order they arrive. If upstream changes either, or adds its own batching of reads, drop this patch rather than port it.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/rpc-ahead.cpp` (build it against the engine's libraries; run it against `ggml-rpc-server` with `GGML_RPC_AHEAD` 0 and 1, straight and through a slow link). `test/run-link-log-test.js` checks the patch is listed.

### 0012-load-progress: how far a brain has loaded
- **Upstream files:** `tools/server/server.cpp` only.
- **Hooks:** `server_run_instance()`: when the server is not run by a router, `ctx_server.set_state_callback()` prints `load_progress: <stage> <percent>` for each loading sample (upstream throttles them to one per 200 ms). Under a router, upstream's own callback is kept.
- **Bob callers:** `loadDots()` in `src/llamacpp.js` reads the lines while a brain loads; `src/brain.js` broadcasts `load-progress` and `src/server.js` sends `cube-load` to the cube.
- **Re-check on rebase:** that `set_state_callback` and the `value` / `current` fields of the loading payload are still there.
### 0013-experts-per-word: more or fewer experts per word, live
- **Upstream files:** `include/llama.h`, `src/llama-cparams.h`, `src/llama-context.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-model.cpp`, `tools/server/server-context.{h,cpp}`, `server-task.h`, `server.cpp`.
- **Hooks:**
  - `llama_cparams` carries a pointer to the context's live counts (`expert_used`, `expert_used_n`) and `expert_used_gen`, which `llm_graph_params::allow_reuse` compares, so a graph is never reused across a change.
  - `llm_graph_context::build_moe_ffn` swaps `n_expert_used` for the live count of that layer before the top-k pick, and the sum of the experts' outputs (`n_expert_used_il`) uses the same count. Not when `selected_experts_in` is given (llama.cpp 1.0 removed warmup, and with it the old "not during warmup" check), and only when `n_expert` is the brain's own.

### 0016-moe-cache-ranking: the expert cache filled at load
- **Environment keys (both off unless set):** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_RANKING=<path of a ranking file>` (a FILE PATH, not a number or a random seed) and `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LOG_EVERY=<N cache updates>`.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp` only.
- **Hooks:** at the end of `llama_moe_cache::impl`'s constructor, after the banks and slot maps are allocated (never with `no_alloc`): `model_fingerprint()` (FNV-1a over each layer's expert tensors: index, type, `ne`, and 64 bytes at the start of the first expert and the end of the last, read with `ggml_backend_tensor_get`) is printed, then `fill_from_ranking()` reads the file. Everything is checked before a slot is touched: the header `bob-moe-ranking 2 <fingerprint> <stamp>`, the fingerprint against this brain, every line in range for this brain (layer < `n_layer`, expert < `n_expert`, count > 0), and the file read to its end. Any failure logs `!!! MoE cache ranking NOT USED: <why> (<path>). The cache fills the stock way instead.` and returns. Then per group it sorts by count, and for slot 0, 1, 2... sets `slot_map[il][e]`, `key_of`, `crf` (the top class), `last_use`, moves the slot to that class's LRU list, counts it in `n_filled`, and copies the expert's bytes from the host tensor into each bank with `ggml_backend_tensor_set`. `GGML_ASSERT`s stop the engine if a ranked layer has no slot map or a slot is already taken (internal faults, not bad files). - **Why:** upstream's cache is reactive: it fills on first use, and with the default `--moe-cache-cpu-share 0` it copies every miss to the card, which thrashed on 1.5.193 (writing 2.5 to 3 times slower; 1.5.198 took it out). Ranked, with the CPU share at 1, the cache holds a fixed set while writing. A ranking from the wrong weights would quietly install a bad placement, worse than none, so it is refused out loud instead.
- **What "fixed" means (read from `plan()`, not yet measured):** with the share at 1, a miss goes to the processor when one of the `n_zero` spare slots (up to `n_expert_used`) is free for every token that picked it. Writing one token at a time always finds one, so nothing is evicted. Batches of 2 to 32 tokens can run out and then copy a miss in, evicting the least-used slot. Batches bigger than 32 don't use the slot map at all, so **prompt reading never gains from a ranking**. The PC check (method addendum 2) turns this into a measurement: writing only, share 1, the `ubatch <= 8` uploaded MiB must stay 0 after the load; with Turbo, small and not zero.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the names `moe_cache_lru`, `slot_map`, `key_of`, `crf`, `last_use`, `move`, `class_of`, `n_filled`, `make_key`, `banks`, `experts`, `stats_small`, `log_stats`, `llama_moe_cache_layer_experts`; that `plan()` still leaves misses to the processor with share 1 and full slots; the `n_class`/`crf` meaning (the top class is `n_class - 1`); and the large-batch bypass (`max_batch`).

### 0019-expert-prefetch: the next layer's experts copied ahead
- **Environment key (off unless set):** `LLAMA_PREFETCH_EXPERTS=1`.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp` and `src/llama-context.h` only.
- **Hooks:** `llama_context::sched_copy_experts`, the copy callback llama gives the scheduler for weights in RAM. After the MoE cache's own check: on first call, `llama_expert_prefetch::init()` maps each RAM expert tensor to the same kind (gate, up, down, gate_up) in the next layer in RAM, opens a second backend on the same device (`ggml_backend_dev_init`) and allocates one staging buffer per kind plus two events. `serve()`: if `src` was copied ahead, the card's stream waits for the `ready` event, copies staging to `dst` on its own stream (`ggml_backend_tensor_copy_async(backend, backend, ...)`), and records `consumed`. At the end of the callback, `ahead()`: when at least half of `src`'s experts were used, the second stream waits for the slot's last `consumed`, then `ggml_backend_tensor_set_async` copies the next tensor whole into staging and records `ready`.
- **Why:** prompt reading with experts in RAM is bound by those copies, which the card's stream does in line with the work. The stock scheduler's prefetch only moves results the other way.
- **Safety:** the staging buffers are ours, not the scheduler's, so no allocator lifetimes change. A staging buffer is never written before the card has moved its last contents out. Any failure to set up (no second stream, no events, no room) logs `!!! expert prefetch NOT USED` and leaves the stock path.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `sched_copy_experts` and `copy_experts_info`, the MoE cache check at its top, `ggml_backend_dev_init`, `ggml_backend_tensor_copy_async`, event support on CUDA and Vulkan.
- **Tests:** none can run without a graphics card. With it on, a CPU build and the trace test still pass (nothing in RAM is offloaded there). The PC test compares reading speed with it on and off and reads its closing line.

### 0020-expert-tiers: warm experts pinned in RAM, cold experts read whole
- **Environment keys (all off unless set):** `LLAMA_EXPERT_READ_WHOLE=1` (cold tier; only "1" turns it on), `LLAMA_EXPERT_READ_WINDOW=N` (default 64), `LLAMA_EXPERT_READ_LOG_EVERY=N`; `LLAMA_EXPERT_WARM_MIB=N` (warm tier) with `LLAMA_EXPERT_WARM_RANKING=<path of a ranking file>`, or `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_RANKING` when that is unset.

- **Hooks:** cold: in `ggml_compute_forward_mul_mat_id` (ggml-cpu.c) and `forward_mul_mat_id` (repack.cpp), on thread 0 right after the rows are grouped by expert (`matrix_row_counts` filled), `ggml_cpu_expert_read_whole(src0, matrix_row_counts, n_as)`. It keeps, per expert tensor (keyed by `src0->data`, in a fixed table of 4096 under `ggml_critical_section_start`), the product number when each expert was last used, and for a picked expert unused for `window` products asks for its whole slice (`src0->data + e*nb[2]`, `nb[2]` bytes, page-aligned) with `PrefetchVirtualMemory` (looked up at run time, Windows 8+) or `madvise(MADV_WILLNEED)`. The locks live until the engine stops.
- **Why:** a brain bigger than RAM is mapped, and the operating system drops and re-reads parts of it page by page as the processor touches them. Pinning keeps the busy RAM experts from being dropped; asking for a whole cold expert turns many small faults into a few large reads.
- **Safety:** neither tier changes a number: the products read the same bytes, only when they come into memory changes (the test checks words and odds are identical). A refused ranking pins nothing and says so (`!!! warm experts NOT PINNED`). Pinning more than the system allows stops early with llama_mlock's own warning. With the hot-experts cache on, the top-ranked experts are both on the card and pinned in RAM; the RAM copy is still read for prompt reading (big batches skip the cache).
- **Re-check on rebase:** the grouping loops and `matrix_row_counts` in both mul_mat_id paths; `llama_mlock`'s `failed_already`; that `tiers_fingerprint` still matches 0016's `model_fingerprint`, and `ffn_gate_up_exps`/`ffn_gate_exps`/`ffn_up_exps`/`ffn_down_exps` on `llama_layer`.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/expert-tiers.py` (tiny made-up brain, CPU build): same words and odds with each tier on and both; the cold tier asks and skips; the warm tier pins the exact number that fit; refusals. ### 0022-lend-cache-to-reading: the cache lent to long reads, back for writing
- **Turned on by:** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND=N` with the MoE cache on (`--moe-cache-mib`), N over 32 and under `n_ubatch`. Off unless set; a value out of range is refused with a `NOT USED` warning.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp`, `src/llama-cparams.h`, `src/llama-moe-cache.{h,cpp}`.
- **Hooks:** the constructor reads the knob right after the MoE cache is made (in the `no_alloc` measuring pass too, so `--fit` sees the smaller step with the cache). `sched_reserve` lays the working memory out for `min(n_ubatch, N)` tokens while the cache is in place (`cparams.moe_cache_lent` false), for `n_ubatch` while lent. In `decode`, before `sched_reserve`: a batch of more than N tokens while not lent, or of 32 or fewer while lent, synchronizes, resets the graph results, frees the scheduler (its compute buffers go first), then `llama_moe_cache::lend()` (frees each device's bank buffer) or `restore()` (clears the tensors' data and buffer, allocates the banks again with `llama_memory_bufs::alloc_buf`, clears them, uploads every filled slot's expert from its host tensor, consecutive slots together, and waits), and reserves again. While lent, `get_slot_map` returns nullptr (no graph gets slots), `copy_experts` copies nothing (big batches upload from RAM), and `copy` asserts it isn't called.
- **Why:** batches over 32 tokens skip the cache (`max_batch`), so its memory is idle during a long read, and the bigger step that memory allows reads faster (the PC: step 6144 with 3.5 GB vs 4096 with 4 GB).
- **Safety:** the LRU state is untouched while lent, so the same experts come back to the same slots: the cache's contents and the words are as if it was never lent. A failed layout after a switch returns -2 from `decode` with the reason in the log. Unproven: the CUDA memory pool grows with the step and never shrinks, so if the bigger step's pool doesn't fit next to the cache, the cache's allocation on the way back fails and that decode returns -2 (the cache stays lent, and the next short write tries again).
- **Re-check on rebase:** `sched_reserve`'s worst-case `n_tokens`; `sched_new`'s reuse check (a null scheduler must make a new one); `max_batch` in the cache; that `ggml_backend_alloc_ctx_tensors_from_buft` still allocates tensors whose data is null and inits their views.
- **Tests:** none on the processor: the MoE cache needs a card. Here: it builds, and `trace-kept.py`, `expert-tiers.py` and `experts-per-word.py` still pass with it off. On the PC (Brain Engine 13, `results/lend-cache-be13`, 12 loads): no failures; words equal to step 6144's at 32K; lent in 78-99 ms, back in 274-286 ms; writing 92.8 vs 88.4 at step 6144 with 3.5 GB.


### 0023-lend-refill-behind: the lent cache refilled behind the writing
- **Turned on by:** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND_BEHIND=K` (1 to 4096) together with 0022's `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND`. Off unless set; out of range is refused with a `NOT USED` warning.

### 0024-cache-read-layout: whole layers in the cache for long reads
- **Turned on by:** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_READ_LAYERS=N` (over 32) with the MoE cache on; ignored when `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND` is set. Off unless set.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp`, `src/llama-cparams.h`, `src/llama-moe-cache.{h,cpp}`.
- **Hooks:** `begin_read_layout()` waits for the devices, saves each group's `key_of` and each cached layer's slot map, then per group gives layers, in order, slots `base .. base + n_expert - 1` while they fit (`resident`), writes the identity into their slot maps and uploads their expert tensors whole (one copy per tensor per layer), and sets `reading`. ### 0025-kq-mask-from-positions: the attention mask made on the card
- **Turned on by:** `LLAMA_KQ_MASK_BUILD=1`. Off unless set; ignored (with a warning) for ALiBi brains.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-kv-cache.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-context.cpp`, `src/llama-cparams.h`.
- **Hooks:** `build_attn_inp_attn_mask` (the one builder for the KV-cache masks: plain, iswa base and sliding window, hybrid) builds the stock table, then, when `kq_mask_can_build` says yes for this ubatch, `build_attn_inp_attn_mask_made` turns it into a made tensor: inputs `kv_pos` (F32 per cell per stream, 1e9 for a hidden cell), `q_pos` (F32 per query row) and `q_rows` (I64 0..n-1); per block of rows `repeat`, `sub`, `scale_bias` + `step` (causal), for standard SWA `scale_bias` + `step` + `mul`, then `log`, then `set_rows` into the table (f32 to f16 there). `can_reuse_attn_mask` refuses a graph whose made/table choice differs from the new ubatch's. `llama_kv_cache::set_input_attn_mask` fills the three inputs (same hide rules as `set_input_attn_mask_impl`: padding, empty, other chat) and skips the table. `place_kq_mask` puts the table and every op on the card that runs all layers (`cparams.kq_mask_backend`, found at load); otherwise the scheduler places them. `graph_max_nodes` gets 1,920 more (3 masks x 64 blocks x 10).
- **Readers of the mask, checked one by one (b12359):** flash attention on CUDA, Vulkan, Metal and CPU, and its `fused_kv_rows` variant (`flash_attn_ext_rows` with `attn_mask.rows`): read the same table. The plain path (`soft_max_ext`, flash attention off): same table in f32. CUDA's tile skip (`flash_attn_mask_to_KV_max`, which looks for minus infinity): same values, so the same tiles are skipped. Not touched, they keep the stock processor-filled table: the no-cache mask (`llm_graph_input_attn_no_cache`, embeddings), cross-attention, the DSA/MLA/lid masks of DeepSeek-style brains, the hybrid-index pool masks. The cube's traces, the expert mask (0001), dials, copy cache and read layout don't read it.
- **Rules it copies, and where it falls back:** a cell is visible when it is in the word's chat, not empty or padding, not later than the word, and (standard SWA) `p1 - p0 < n_swa`. Falls back to the stock table, per ubatch: non-causal attention, 2-D (M-RoPE vision) positions, chunked or symmetric SWA, a word in two chats, a stream with more than one chat. Positions are whole numbers far below 2^24, so f32 holds them exactly; `step(x)` is `x > 0`, `log(1) = 0`, `log(0) = -inf` on every backend.
- **Why:** at 128K and 256K the pinned `CUDA_Host compute` buffer grows by the table (1 to 4 GB of RAM the PC uses for experts), the table is copied to the card every reading step (2 GB a step at 256K, step 4096), and the processor fills it first. Calibrate's context-sizes run (Addendum 40) found this was the 128K/256K "spill".
- **What it doesn't save:** the table on the card. Saving that too needs flash attention to work out the boundary from positions inside its kernels (every CUDA variant, the tile skip, the CPU fallback): our own ggml-cuda build, which the Brain Engine overlay doesn't ship today.
- **Safety:** every row of the table is written before attention reads it (the blocks cover all rows, and `set_rows` chains them, so attention depends on the last one). Blocks are at most 64 MiB of f32 unless that needs more than 64 blocks.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the hide rules in `set_input_attn_mask_impl` (any new rule needs a fallback in `kq_mask_can_build` or a new op); `build_attn_inp_attn_mask`'s shapes; callers of `build_attn_inp_attn_mask` (new ones need `place_kq_mask`).
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/mask-made.py` (processor): a tiny MoE brain and a tiny Gemma 2 shaped brain with a 6-word window; flash on/off x one cache/one per chat x 1/3 chats x step 512/7; each run reads prompts, writes 24 words side by side, makes a hole and reads into it, shifts every chat back 5, copies a chat and writes in it; the odds of the made mask and the stock table must be equal bit for bit, and the made mask must be used. 32 of 32 pass. ### 0030-table-window: big lookup tables read unbuffered, in a window of their own
- **Environment keys (off unless set):** `LLAMA_TABLE_DIRECT=1` (only "1" turns it on); `LLAMA_TABLE_DIRECT_MIB=N` (the window's size, default 512; a fraction is fine).
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-model.cpp` (after the tensors are loaded), `src/llama-impl.cpp` (`llama_prefetch_rows`), `src/llama-model-loader.{h,cpp}` (the path of each file), `src/CMakeLists.txt`; new `src/llama-table-window.{h,cpp}`.
- **Hook:** for every tensor upstream reads on demand and prefetches (`can_prefetch`: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next's n-gram table, DeepSeek V4.1's Engram, Gemma 4's per-layer embeddings), the tensor's data is pointed at a reserved, empty stretch of memory as big as the table. Before every gather, the brain's own code already calls `llama_prefetch_rows` with the rows it needs. With a window, that call reads just those 4 KiB pages through `llama_file` with unbuffered I/O (`O_DIRECT` on Linux and Android, `FILE_FLAG_NO_BUFFERING` on Windows; Mac falls back to buffered reads and says so). When the window is full, the pages used least recently are given back.
- **Why:** these tables are 28 to 54 GB. Read through the memory map, their pages fill the system's file cache and push the brain's experts out of RAM. Unbuffered, they take at most the window. The idea is Strata's (`--ple-io direct`, github Jackwwg83/Strata-DS, MIT); the code is ours.
- **Safety:** a page that is given back is made unreadable, so a gather that reads a row nobody asked for stops the engine instead of reading zeros. Every brain that has such a table asks for its rows first. Any failure to set up (no file path, no address space, no buffer) logs `!!! table window NOT USED` and keeps the memory map. A failed read stops the engine with the error.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `can_prefetch`, `llama_prefetch_rows` and its callers in `qwen4exp.cpp`, `deepseek41.cpp` and `gemma4.cpp` (every gather must be preceded by a prefetch of the same rows); `llama_file`'s direct I/O.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/table-window.py`, with a tiny Flash-Next shaped brain from llama.cpp's own `test-llama-archs`. The same words as the memory map with a big window, a tiny one that keeps giving pages back, the brain read whole (`--load-mode none`) and a small step; off when the key is 0. Windows: compiled by the engine build, unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0031-moe-cache-learn: the hot-experts cache learns while writing
- **Environment keys (off unless set):** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEARN=N` (words per layer between promotions; 0 or unset is off); `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEARN_MARGIN=M` (how much busier a missed expert must be than the one it replaces, default 2, at least 1). ### 0032-moe-cache-cpu-read: prompts read through the cache, misses on the processor
- **Environment key (off unless set):** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_CPU_READ=N` (33 to 16384 tokens). Anything else logs `!!! LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_CPU_READ=... NOT USED`.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp` (`get_slot_map`, `get_cpu_map`, `copy()`, the zero slots), `src/llama-graph.cpp` (`build_moe_ffn`).
- **Hook:** a reading batch of 33 to N tokens on a cached layer now goes through the cache's slot map with a CPU part, as a written word with `--moe-cache-cpu-share` does: the card reads the cached experts where they are, and the processor computes every miss from RAM. Nothing is copied to the card for that layer, and the cache's experts don't change (the misses never come in). The CPU part's expert products are pinned to the processor, so a card that would take big batches over (CUDA does from 32 tokens) doesn't copy the experts for them. The zero slots go up to 64 so many misses can meet in one token-heavy batch; a miss that still finds none is copied in the stock way. Works with any `--moe-cache-cpu-share`, 0 included.
- **Why:** stock, a prompt over 32 tokens copies every expert it uses to the card for each step (cached ones card to card, the rest over PCIe). For a short or medium prompt each expert serves few words, so copying it costs more than letting the processor read it from RAM where it is. Strata measured 19 to 35% faster prompts under 1,024 tokens doing the same (github Jackwwg83/Strata-DS, MIT; their numbers, other hardware); the code is ours.
- **Costs:** 64 zero slots of card memory per group (64 experts' worth taken from `--moe-cache-mib`). On a real card the processor's sums can differ from the card's in the last bits, as with the CPU share. For long reads the card is likely faster: the Calibrate grid should find the N where it stops paying.
- **Log:** `prompts read with misses on the CPU: hits, misses (on the CPU), uploaded` beside the other cache lines.
- **Fixed Nov 19 (after BE19):** BE19 pinned only the last expert product (down) of the CPU part to the processor; on a real card gate and up were still offloaded with ids of -1 for experts not on the processor, and the engine stopped (`GGML_ASSERT(id >= 0 && id < n_expert)` in the expert copy) at every prompt of 33 to N tokens. All three are pinned now. With `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LOG_EVERY` set, each built layer logs `N expert products of the CPU part pinned to the processor`, and the test needs 3 (the BE19 code gives 1).
- **Re-check on rebase:** `build_moe_cache_slots`, the CPU part in `build_moe_ffn`, the scheduler's offload rule (`1.off`).
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/moe-cpu-read.py` on the stand-in card: with share 0 and 1, the prompt goes through the cache, every miss on the processor, 0 MiB uploaded, the same words as with it off; a value of 16 says NOT USED. The stand-in card never takes batches over, so the pin is only proven on CUDA by the run on the PC: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0033-sched-overlap-log: does the processor already work while the card does?
- **Environment key (off unless set):** `GGML_SCHED_OVERLAP_LOG=N` (print every N graphs of each kind). With `GGML_SCHED_TIMING` on it logs `!!! GGML_SCHED_OVERLAP_LOG NOT USED`, because the timing log waits for every piece.
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/src/ggml-backend.cpp` (`ggml_backend_sched_compute_splits`, `ggml_backend_sched_new`).
- **What we found first:** upstream already has the overlap Strata gets from its "doorbell". When the processor computes part of a layer's experts (`--moe-cache-cpu-share`), the card's piece with the cached experts is queued without waiting, the processor's inputs are fetched ahead on the card's queue with an event (`ggml_backend_sched_prefetch_inputs`), and the processor starts as soon as those arrive, while the card still works. On the stand-in card 4 of every 5 processor pieces start this way. So the scheduler rewrite or new CUDA op we scoped isn't needed to get the overlap.
- **Hook:** host-side clocks only, no extra waits: how long processor pieces compute, how long they wait for the card's results, how many were fetched ahead, and how long the card piece after a processor piece waits before it can start.
- **How to read it on the PC:** processor waiting large means the overlap isn't happening (find which input isn't fetched ahead). Card waiting large with processor computing about the same means the processor is the slow side, so a smaller share or 0032's limit is the lever. Both small means the turns cost little, and Strata's remaining edge is elsewhere: fewer pieces per word, and CUDA graphs over a whole word (Strata captures one; ours are cut at every processor piece).
- **Log:** `overlap, writing (1 token): N graphs, per graph X ms queued: P processor pieces (A with inputs fetched while the card worked), processor computing ..., processor waiting for the card ..., card waiting after the processor ...`.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/sched-overlap.py` on the stand-in card: the same words on and off, pieces counted, some fetched ahead, NOT USED with the timing log. Windows and CUDA: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0034-spec-gate: drafts only while they pay
- **Environment key (off unless set):** `LLAMA_SPEC_GATE=rate` (between 0 and 1, for example 0.4). Anything else logs `!!! LLAMA_SPEC_GATE=... NOT USED`.

- **Found on the way:** on the tiny Flash-Next shaped brain, upstream's n-gram drafting by itself changes the words at temperature 0 (its batch of several words computes slightly differently from one word at a time). The gate doesn't add to that; with it on, these replies match writing without drafts.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the draft loop in `server-context.cpp` (where `get_n_draft_max` is called, and the check after `accepted`).
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/spec-gate.py` (llama-server on the tiny brain): drafts mostly thrown away without the gate; with it, pauses, growing pauses, far fewer drafts but not none, the same words twice; NOT USED for 2. ### 0035-context-change: a new context size without loading the brain again
- **Switch:** a request, not a key: `POST /context` with `{"n_ctx": N}` and/or `{"moe_cache_mib": M}`. Nothing changes until something asks, so it is off by default. Servers without the patch answer 404, and Bob falls back to a restart.
- **Upstream files:** `common/common.{h,cpp}` (`common_init_result`: `keep_model_for_next`, the placement check), `tools/server/server-context.{h,cpp}`, `server-task.h`, `server.cpp` (the route and its task).
- **Hook:** the request waits until no slot is busy. Then the server frees its context the way it does when it goes to sleep, but hands the loaded brain over instead of freeing it, and loads again with the new size. If the brain's placement is the same as before (same files, layers on the card, devices, overrides, load mode), the brain is used as it is and only the context is made again: the KV cache, the hot-experts cache at its new size, the slots, the drafter. If `--fit` puts layers elsewhere for the new size, the brain is loaded again. That's still faster than a restart: the file is in RAM already, and Bob's server, port and settings stay. If the new size fails, the old one is made again and the request returns an error.
- **What it costs:** the chats' KV caches are emptied. Bob saves and restores slots around it to keep them.
- **Not yet:** moving single layers off the card without loading the brain again. The weights of all layers on a card share one buffer, so one layer can't be freed on its own. That needs one buffer per layer at load time, and a move that points a layer's weights back at the mapped file. About 1 to 2 weeks; scoped, not built. Until then, a bigger context that doesn't fit is handled in this order: Bob first shrinks the hot-experts cache in the same request (no reload); only if that isn't enough does `--fit` move layers, with the brain loaded again.
- **Log:** `brain kept loaded: only the context is made again` or `brain loaded again: its placement changed`, then `context A -> B tokens in X s`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `common_init_result`'s constructor and its impl's member order, the server's `destroy()` and `load_model()` (the same path as waking from sleep).
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/ctx-change.py` (llama-server on the tiny brain): 1024 -> 4096 -> 1024. A long prompt refused at 1024 then gives the words of a server started at 4096, the same words after shrinking, the brain kept both times (about 0.02 s each here), an empty request refused. Also checked by hand on the stand-in card with the hot-experts cache resized (3 -> 6 MiB, same words, 0.28 s). Windows and CUDA: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0036-kv-split: the chat memory split between the card and RAM
- **Environment key (off unless set):** `LLAMA_KV_SPLIT=N` (rounded up to a multiple of 256). It works with one chat at a time and flash attention on. It logs `!!! LLAMA_KV_SPLIT=... NOT USED: why` for: flash attention off, more than one chat (`-np` above 1), `-nkvo`, MLA brains, caches that share cells, indexer caches, sliding-window caches, a context no bigger than N, and no layer on a card.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-kv-cache.{h,cpp}` (the split cache), `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}` (the attention input and `build_attn`).
- **Hook:** each card layer's K and V live whole in RAM (the card's pinned RAM when it has some), plus a copy of the first N cells on the card. Every word is written to both; a word after cell N goes to one spare cell at the end of the card's copy, so the copy never needs more than N + 1 cells. Attention then reads one of three ways:
  1. The chat fits in N: the card's copy only, as without the split.
  2. A prompt past N: the RAM copy, which the scheduler uploads to the card for that layer, and the card attends.
  3. Writing one word past N: the card attends to cells before N, the processor to the rest, and the two are merged. Each part first works out its log-sum-exp; that becomes the other part's sink, so each part's answer comes out already weighted by its share, and their sum is the exact attention. It costs one extra pass over K on each side.
  Attention rotation for quantized K and V, and K/V scales, are applied before the cache and after the attention, so they work as before. A layer stored some other way (a model's own attention code) is marked and reads RAM from then on. A saved chat that is loaded again refreshes the card's copy. Context shift is off while split (it would move the RAM copy only).
- **Why:** past N, the card holds only N cells of chat memory per layer, so a long context costs RAM instead of card memory, and the card's room goes to layers or the expert cache. Strata keeps 32K cells on the card and the rest in RAM, uploaded per layer while a prompt is read (github Jackwwg83/Strata-DS, MIT; their numbers: prompts 4 to 5% faster at 32K and 64K, no gain while writing). The merge while writing is ours, and so is the code.
- **What to expect:** unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test on a real card. Qwen 3.6's chat memory at 256K is about 2.7 GB at q8_0 and 1.4 GB at q4_0, so on the PC it may only pay where the card is full. Writing past N runs part of each attention layer on the processor, which may be slower; the run plan measures it.
- **Log:** `KV split: the first N of M cells of each card layer on the card, all of them in RAM`, then once each: `a batch past cell N reads the RAM copy on the card` and `writing past cell N: the card attends to its copy and the processor to the rest, merged`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the KV cache constructor's per-layer buffers, `cpy_k`/`cpy_v`, `state_read_sinfo`, `build_attn_inp_kv_impl`, `build_attn` for `llm_graph_input_attn_kv`, and both `set_input`/`can_reuse` of the plain and hybrid attention inputs.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/kv-split.py` (a tiny brain on a stand-in card, N = 256, context 2,048): the same words as the split turned off at f16, q8_0 and q4_0, a prompt past N, writing that crosses N, a chat that never leaves the card copy; Qwen 3.5 / 3.6's own kind of brain (recurrent layers between attention ones, scaled K and V) at f16 and q8_0; llama-server with a saved chat erased, written over and restored; NOT USED for flash attention off, two chats, `-nkvo` and a context no bigger than N. 28 of 28 pass. Turning the merge 3% wrong fails 5 checks, and leaving out the refresh fails the restore check, so the checks can see a fault. On the stand-in the card and the processor compute alike; on a real card expect close odds, not always the same words. CUDA and Windows: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0039-kv-tiers: Progressive Memory (the newest words kept sharper)
- **Environment key (off unless set):** `LLAMA_KV_TIERS="q8_0:8192,q4_0:65536"`: up to 4 steps of `type:window`, sharpest first, windows growing. The main cache keeps every word at its own type (`-ctk`/`-ctv`, e.g. q3_0); each step keeps the newest `window` words again at its type. `LLAMA_KV_TIERS_SLACK` (default 8192, rounded up to 256) is extra room in each ring for a batch. It logs `!!! LLAMA_KV_TIERS=... NOT USED: why` for: flash attention off, more than one chat (`-np` above 1), MLA brains, caches that share cells, indexer caches, sliding-window caches, the chat memory split (0036) set too, the position-made mask (0025 / 0026 keys) set, steps out of order or a bad type, and a window as big as the context.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-kv-cache.{h,cpp}` (the rings, their position tags and masks), `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}` (the attention input and `build_attn`), `src/llama-context.cpp` (graph room; the fused mask-rows path is off while tiers are set), `ggml-cpu/ops.cpp` and `ggml-cuda/{cpy.cu,ggml-cuda.cu}` (copying a quantized tensor straight to f16).
- **Hook:** each step is a ring of window + slack cells per layer; position p goes to slot p mod ring, and the cache remembers which position sits in each slot. Every word is written to the main cache and to every ring from the same full-precision K and V, so nothing is rounded twice. Attention reads each old word from the sharpest copy that still has it:
  1. Writing one word: the main cache (with the words a ring holds cut from its mask) and each ring attend on their own, each with a sink far below every score so an empty part gives 0. Each part's log-sum-exp comes from the same scores; the softmax of those is each part's share, and the weighted sum is exact attention over the mixed copies. 0039 worked each log-sum-exp out from a second pass over the part's K, which made writing slow in long chats; since 0045 each part's flash attention hands it back, so the main cache is read once a word (see 0045).
  2. Reading a batch no bigger than the slack: the main cache is read as f16 and each ring's copies of the newest words are laid over it (widest step first, so the sharpest wins), then one plain attention.
  3. A batch bigger than the slack: the rings are written but the main cache is read; writing is sharp again after it.
  Rotation of quantized K and V and K/V scales are applied before the cache and after the attention, as before. The rings forget on a cleared chat, erased or shifted words, and a loaded chat (a restored chat reads the main cache until new words arrive).
- **Why:** the owner's idea (Nov 19): "should context compress from q4 to q3 as it stretches out", then 2 or 3 steps like 8 to 4 to 3, for every brain; named Progressive Memory. Words near the end of a chat get most of the attention, so keeping them sharp should hold most of the quality of the sharp type at close to the room of the small one. The idea of keeping recent words at higher precision is not new: KIVI and SKVQ keep a recent window in full precision, and PM-KVQ (arXiv 2505.18610) and KVmix lower precision step by step for older words. Ours keeps every word in the small type as well (so no word is ever re-rounded), and the exact merge while writing is ours, and so is the code.
- **What to expect:** room: the main cache at its type plus each ring (window + slack) at its type. With q3_0 underneath and `q8_0:8192,q4_0:65536` on Qwen 3.6 35B at 256K, that is about 1.65 GB (1.1 GB main, 0.17 GB for the q8_0 ring, 0.39 GB for the q4_0 ring), against 1.4 GB for plain q4_0 and 1.1 GB for plain q3_0, with the newest 8K words at q8_0 and the newest 64K at q4_0 or better. Speed: mode 1 did one more K pass per step while writing, and mode 2 makes an f16 copy of each layer's K and V per batch. The PC (Nov 20) found writing 3 to 4.5 times slower than plain q3_0 from 32K to 256K, with reading and the first word within 3%; 0045 takes the extra pass away. Drift should sit between the steps' type and the main type, nearer the sharp one for most jobs; the run plan measures it.
- **Log:** `Progressive Memory: every word at K/V, again at T for the newest W ...`, then once each: `reading: the newest words' sharp copies laid over the cache`, `writing: each word attends to the sharpest copy of every word, merged`, and `a batch bigger than LLAMA_KV_TIERS_SLACK reads the main cache only`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the KV cache constructor and `apply_ubatch`, `prepare`, `seq_rm`/`seq_add`/`seq_div`, `state_read`, `build_attn_inp_kv_impl`, `build_attn` for `llm_graph_input_attn_kv`, both `set_input`/`can_reuse` of the plain and hybrid attention inputs, and `graph_max_nodes`.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/kv-tiers.py` (a tiny brain, head size 64, on the processor and the stand-in card): an f16 step holding the whole chat gives f16's words over a q3_0 cache, on both; a q8_0 step over a q8_0 cache gives the plain words (the merge is exact); three steps (f16, q8_0, then q3_0) answer with the same words on both; a batch bigger than the slack; four NOT USED reasons; drift q8_0 < q3_0 with a q8_0 step < plain q3_0. 15 of 15 pass. The exact checks turn rotation off: rotated numbers rounded to f16 differ by a hair, and the random brain has near-ties a hair flips. The CUDA files build for sm_120 here (pip nvcc); no real card has run them. Windows, CUDA on a card and Android: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0040-moe-cpu-round: the expert cache's processor part rounds like the card
- **On by default** (a reliability fix: it changes no setting, only how close the processor's numbers are to the card's). `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_CPU_ROUND=0` turns it off, for comparing. It logs `the expert cache's processor part rounds like the card (blocks of 32): on` once.
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ggml-cpu.c` (a second path for `GGML_OP_MUL_MAT_ID`), `src/llama-graph.cpp` (`build_moe_ffn`, where 0032 pins the CPU part).
- **Hook:** 0032 pins the expert cache's CPU part (gate, up and down) to the processor; it now also marks those three products (`op_params[0] = 1`). A marked product over k-quant weights (Q2_K to Q6_K, any type whose processor input is q8_K) rounds its input to q8_0, blocks of 32 values with one scale each, as a CUDA card rounds to q8_1 for the same weights, instead of q8_K's blocks of 256. Each weight row is turned into floats once and dotted with every word routed to that expert. Types whose input is already q8_0 (Q4_0, Q8_0 and the like) take the usual path. Made-up numbers: the 256 rounding's error is 1.3 to 2.6 times the 32 one's, and it is most of the gap between the two. The owner's ask (Nov 19): "the processor should round and add like the card". The adding was already the same (the two parts are summed slot by slot, each zero where the other has the expert).
- **What to expect:** the processor's misses now come out as the card would have made them, up to the order of the sums (the last bits). Speed: the processor part does float dot products instead of 8-bit ones, so it may be slower; how much, and whether 0032 still pays, is unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test. LLAMA_MOE_LOOKAHEAD NOT USED`, since misses go to the processor). ### 0042-moe-doorbell: the card's experts and the processor's misses at the same time, joined once a layer
- **Off by default.** `LLAMA_MOE_DOORBELL=1` with the hot-experts cache and `--moe-cache-cpu-share` above 0, on one NVIDIA card (Volta or newer, CUDA; HIP and MUSA build it as unsupported). Elsewhere it logs `!!! LLAMA_MOE_DOORBELL NOT USED` (share 0, more than one card, more than 64 experts a word, or a card that can't ring it) and the old path runs unchanged. Logs `the MoE cache rings a doorbell while writing` once and `doorbell: N rings, M with processor work, K misses brought in a word later (...)` with the cache totals. `LLAMA_MOE_DOORBELL_TIMEOUT_MS` (default 10000) is how long the card waits for the processor; `LLAMA_MOE_DOORBELL_CPU_RING=1` (the processor's own ring, on any card) and `LLAMA_MOE_DOORBELL_STALL=1` (the processor never answers) are for tests.
- **Upstream files:** `ggml/include/ggml.h`, `ggml/src/ggml.c` (the step `GGML_OP_MOE_DOORBELL`, its two forms `ggml_moe_doorbell_ring` and `ggml_moe_doorbell_join`, and the shared memory layout `struct ggml_moe_doorbell`), `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ops.cpp/.h`, `ggml-cpu.c` (the processor's version), `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/moe-doorbell.cu/.cuh` (new) and `ggml-cuda.cu` (dispatch, supports_op, two proc addresses for the mapped memory), `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/ggml-rpc.cpp` and `ggml/include/ggml-rpc.h` (the step is on the wire list but never sent: the client says it can't run it and the server refuses it), `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp/.h` (`db_*`), `src/llama-graph.cpp` (`build_moe_ffn`), `src/llama-context.cpp` (`process_ubatch`), `common/arg.cpp` (help line).
- **Hook:** once every cache group is full (until then the old path uploads as before), a written batch of 1 to 8 tokens builds each cached layer as: **ring** (on the card: slot = mirror[pick] if cached, else zero slot k for the k-th pick; it also copies the picks and the expert input into mapped pinned memory and adds one to `seq`), the cached experts through those slots, then **join** (one thread waits for `done == seq`, with a time limit, then each pick's row adds the processor's result where the pick was a miss, + 0 elsewhere, as the old `add` did). No copy callback and no CPU split: the layer is card work only, so the host queues the whole word ahead. The mirror is a device copy of each layer's slot map, changed only between words. The processor's part is a small graph per layer and batch size, made once from the same `build_experts` as the old CPU part (by swapping `ctx0`), its inputs and result in the shared memory, its products marked like 0040; a thread of the cache spins on `seq` while a word is written, computes it on its own processor backend and threads, runs the cache's bookkeeping (plan, LRU, 0031's learning, the trace) and sets `done`. What the bookkeeping decides to bring in is kept in `db_pending` and goes up in `doorbell_between_words`, called before every batch, which also waits for the card (so the thread has answered every ring), makes the mirrors again when the slot maps changed, wakes the thread and drops the graphs when the doorbell goes on or off. The kept picks for the expert trace come from the graph's own copy (no lookup split to move). The card side uses kernels, not events (an event recorded while a CUDA graph is captured is dropped), with system-wide fences and volatile or uncached reads. On a timeout the join kernel flags it in the shared memory; a watchdog thread stops the program with the message within 5 ms, and the kernel traps after 5 s if nothing did.
- **Why:** with share 1 each cached layer stopped the card twice: in the copy callback (the picks read back and the plan made while the card had nothing queued) and before the add (the host computed the processor's part, then queued the rest). Strata (MIT) found the overlap, and that `cudaEventRecord` inside graph capture is dropped. Design: `doorbell-overlap-design.md`, plan A.
- **What to expect:** words: with the share at 1, a full cache and learning off, the same products in the same order, so the same words as share 1 without it. With learning on (or a share below 1) an expert that comes in does so a word later, so the words can differ from today's and need the drift gate. Speed: the stops per cached layer go, and the processor's work overlaps the card's; how much that saves on the PC is unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test. ### 0043-moe-standins: an expert already on the card plays a missed one while writing (lossy, Turbo only)
- **Off by default, and lossy.** The words change, so only Bob's Turbo mode is meant to set it. `LLAMA_MOE_STANDIN=rank|near|drop` with the hot-experts cache (any `--moe-cache-cpu-share`); `LLAMA_MOE_STANDIN_MAX=K` (default 2, clamped to 1 .. experts-a-word minus 1). Any other value logs `!!! LLAMA_MOE_STANDIN=... NOT USED` and runs as off. On, it logs `!!! MoE stand-ins ON` once, `stand-ins, words A-B: N, X a word, most M in a word, most P in a layer for a word` every 64 words (-lv 4), `stand-ins: word W layer L: n (miss->stand-in ...)` per layer (-lv 5), and `stood in (way, at most K a layer a word): ...` with the cache totals. Stand-ins are not counted as hits there.
- **The ways.** `rank` is Strata-HotRod's (MIT): the cached expert of the layer the router scored best for this word, not already picked. `near`: the cached expert whose router column (`ffn_gate_inp`) is most like the missed one's by cosine; the 64 closest of each expert are worked out once at load, in a few threads. `drop`: no stand-in, the miss counts for nothing (the slot of the expert `rank` would pick is computed with factor 0, so nothing is copied for it).
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-graph.cpp` (`build_moe_ffn`: two small blocks, before and after `build_moe_cache_slots`), `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp/.h` (new functions `sp_*`, `get_standin_scores`, `get_standin_scale`; three small hooks in the copy callback). Nothing else, so it stays clear of 0042.
- **Hook:** in a one-word graph (softmax or sigmoid router, no expert groups, not Llama 4 or GroveMoE, picks made here), the router scores before the gating are copied to a small device tensor of the cache, and after the slot lookup the picks' weights are multiplied by `get_rows(factor, slots)`, a per-layer F32 factor read by slot (1 unless a stand-in played; read through the slots, so it always comes after the callback that sets it). In the copy callback, with the picks, the cache reads those scores (same sync); before the plan it chooses up to K stand-ins (misses, lightest first, never the top pick, each stand-in on the card and not picked) and puts them in the ids, so the plan sees hits and copies nothing for them; after the plan it sends the card a slot map with the stand-in's slot under the missed expert (its own slot map is unchanged: the miss is still not on the card) and sets the factors: stand-in weight / missed weight, times the word's total over the new total; the factors of the last stand-in go back to 1 first. With every expert cached nothing stands in and the factors stay 1, so the words are the same as off.
- **Why:** on the PC a third of each written word is copying missed experts (BE19/BE21 where-time). Look-ahead (0041) tries to hide the copies; stand-ins skip some of them at a cost in answers. HotRod gates each stand-in by router weight x logit gap against a budget it learns and re-checks; this patch has only the plain cap K, so it is cruder.
- **What to expect:** fewer copies a word, at most K a layer; the answers drift. On the stand-in brain (random weights, 16 experts, 4 a word, 4 layers), K = 2 over ~441 scored tokens: top-1 same as off 57% (rank), 51% (near), 49% (drop), mean KL 1.5 / 1.9 / 2.1. Those numbers say nothing about a real brain. Speed and drift on the PC (RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB, Qwen3.6 35B MoE): unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `build_moe_ffn` router and weights blocks, the copy callback (ids read, plan, slot map send), `moe_cache_lru::plan`.
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/moe-standins.py` (stand-in card over ggml-rpc): off gives the very same words as the build before 0043 (third argument: its bin folder), as `off` and as a bad value; with every expert cached, each way gives the same words as off and no stand-in; each way at K = 1 and 2 runs, logs per word and layer and never more than K in a layer for a word; the -lv 4 count every 64 words; each way also runs with share 1; llama-perplexity `--kl-divergence` (one-word batches) off vs off is the floor (100%, KL 0), then each way at K = 1 and 2. 40 of 40 pass. moe-lookahead, moe-learn and moe-cpu-read still pass. `--help` names its keys under `--moe-cache-mib`, beside 0041's. CUDA build and a real card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0044-kv-q3-fast-reads: 3-bit chat memory read faster in flash attention (CUDA)

### 0045-kv-tiers-fast-writing: Progressive Memory writes without a second pass over the main cache
- **Only with Progressive Memory (`LLAMA_KV_TIERS`, 0039), which stays off unless set; the same numbers up to float rounding.** Nothing else changes: without the key, and for batches (0039's modes 2 and 3), the graph is the same as before. `LLAMA_KV_TIERS_EXPLICIT=1` is for tests: it takes 0039's way for every part. `--help` says so under `-ctv`, next to 0039's line on `LLAMA_KV_TIERS` (`common/arg.cpp`). It logs once `writing: each part's share comes from its own flash attention (the main cache read once a word)`, or `writing: some parts' shares come from one more pass over their K (...)` when a part fell back. The cache size line now ends with each ring's room: `llama_kv_cache: size = ... V (q3_0): N MiB, sharp rings (q8_0): M MiB`.
- **Why:** the PC's speed test (Nov 20, RTX 5070 Ti 16 GB, Qwen 3.6 35B, CUDA, flash attention on), writing t/s, plain q3_0 against Progressive 3-step / q3_0 + `q8_0:8192`: 32K 87.3 against 30.8 / 37.8; 128K 47.3 against 11.1 / 14.1; 256K 29.7 against 6.5 / 7.5, with reading and the first word within 3%. 0039's one-word merge worked each part's log-sum-exp out from the part's scores: a plain matrix product of the whole quantized main cache with the question every word (on CUDA that is the main cache dequantized and multiplied, every layer), then max, exp, sum and log. That cost grows with the chat.
- **The choice:** of the ways looked at, a flash attention that also hands back the log-sum-exp is the one that is exact and costs nothing: the kernels keep each row's max and sum anyway (the CPU code per row and per chunk, the CUDA vector kernel per block, for its combine). Using one part's log-sum-exp as the other's sink (as 0036 does) gives the main part already weighted, but the rings' weights still need the main cache's sum, so one pass over it would remain.
- **Hook:** a new ggml call `ggml_flash_attn_ext_with_lse` (op param 5 = 1) makes the result one slice longer in dim 3; `ggml_flash_attn_ext_get_out` is the usual result and `ggml_flash_attn_ext_get_lse` the `[n_head, n_batch]` log-sum-exps (natural log, sink included, in the units of scale x KQ + mask). The CPU backend writes it in all three of its paths (one row at a time, tiled batches, KV split in chunks; the tiled path now also moves its max when the sink is bigger, which only the log-sum-exp reads). CUDA writes it only through the vector kernel (1 or 2 query rows, head size 64/128/256, K/V a multiple of 256 long): with one block per row the kernel writes its `[max, sum]` when given somewhere to, and a small kernel (`flash_attn_write_lse`) folds the blocks' pairs into the log-sum-exp after the combine. `ggml_backend_dev_supports_op` refuses the op on every back end but the processor, CUDA and card sharing (Metal, Vulkan, SYCL, ROCm, MUSA and the rest would leave the slice unwritten), and card sharing says yes only for the shapes a CUDA card on the far side can do. In `build_attn` (mode 1) each part is asked with a stand-in node in a scratch context whether its back end can do it; a part that can't (or every part with `LLAMA_KV_TIERS_EXPLICIT=1`) takes 0039's pass, so a merge can mix the two ways and stays exact.
- **Upstream files:** `common/arg.cpp` (one help line), `ggml/include/ggml.h`, `ggml/src/ggml.c` (the new call and views), `ggml/src/ggml-backend.cpp` (the refusal), `ggml/src/ggml-cpu/ops.cpp` (and one line in `ggml-cpu/spacemit/ime.cpp`, whose own kernel leaves it to the common code), `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/fattn.cu`, `fattn-common.cuh`, `fattn-vec.cuh`, `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/ggml-rpc.cpp`, `src/llama-graph.{h,cpp}` (`build_attn_mha` takes an optional `lse` out, `build_attn`'s mode 1), `src/llama-kv-cache.cpp` (the size line).
- **What to expect:** the one-word step reads the main cache once, as plain q3_0 does, plus each ring once, so writing should come close to plain q3_0's speed plus the rings' small share (a ring of 8K + 8K slack against a main cache of 128K or 256K). ### 0046-kv-q2: a 2-bit chat memory type (q2_kv)
- **Switch (off unless asked for):** `-ctk q2_kv -ctv q2_kv`, or one side only (`-ctk q4_0 -ctv q2_kv`). Also through 0037's `POST /context` (`cache_type_k`, `cache_type_v`). Nothing uses it unless one of those names it. The target setting is "Progressive 8+2": `-ctk q2_kv -ctv q2_kv` with `LLAMA_KV_TIERS=q8_0:8192`, so the newest 8K words are kept again at q8_0 (0039, 0045).
- **The name:** upstream already has a `q2_0` (type 42), a brain-file type with blocks of 64 and levels -1, 0, 1, 2. It can't be changed without breaking brain files, so the chat memory's own 2-bit type is `q2_kv` (type 45). `-ctk q2_0` is still refused, as before.
- **The type:** blocks of 32 values, one f16 scale d and 32 two-bit codes (four a byte): 10 bytes, against q4_0's 18 and q3_0's 14, so the chat memory is 44% smaller than q4_0 (K q4_0 + V q2_kv: 22%). Four levels centred on zero: code c is (2c - 3) times d/2, that is -1.5, -0.5, 0.5 and 1.5 times d, and each value rounded to the nearest level (against d as stored, in f16). There is no zero level. The writer tries d = the largest size in the block / 1.5, 1.7, 1.9 and 2.2 (`GGML_Q2_KV_DIVISORS` in `ggml-common.h`) and keeps the one with the least squared error over the block, the first on a tie: 1.5 puts the largest value on the outer level, the others clip it to fit the rest better. On Gaussian (rotated-like) blocks every block took a smaller d than / 1.5 in the check, and the error fell from 0.45 of the signal to 0.34. A block with one big outlier among small values also clips it: least squares favours the 31 small values, so the outlier comes back at 0.68 of its size (on all 2,000 such made-up blocks); keeping it whole would take a different measure than squared error (unproven whether keys need that; the PC's perplexity and needle tests would show it). Upstream's rotation of quantized K and V (`attn_rot`) applies to it as to q4_0.
- **CUDA reading:** the flash attention vector kernel spreads a byte's four codes into four bytes with 0044's masks and shifts (`q3_0_spread_lo`), doubles them and takes 3 from each byte (`__vsub4`), so the K dot is one `dp4a` against the q8_1 Q times d/2, and V is the same four bytes times d/2. The numbers are the processor's: the dequantizer (copies, long prompts, Progressive Memory's f16 copies) gives (2c - 3) x (d/2) bit for bit, a half V is that rounded to f16 once, and the card's writing (set_rows) tries the same scales and rounds every step on its own (`__fdiv_rn`, `__frcp_rn`, `__fmul_rn`, `__fadd_rn`, `__fsub_rn`; the CUDA files are built with fast math and fused multiply-adds), as the processor's writer does (each product through a `volatile`, so no compiler fuses it), so the card and the processor pick the same d and codes. Values small enough to be flushed to zero by fast math (below 1e-38) are the one place they could differ.
- **Upstream files:** as 0038: ggml-base (`ggml.h` enum 45 and the count, `ggml-common.h` block, `ggml-quants.{c,h}` reference quantize and dequantize, `ggml.c` traits), the CPU backend (`ggml-cpu.c` traits, `quants.{c,h}` the row quantizer and a plain C dot product with q8_0, `ops.cpp` every place q3_0 is listed), CUDA (`common.cuh`, `dequantize.cuh`, `convert.cu`, `cpy-utils.cuh`, `set-rows.cu`, `getrows.cu`, `ggml-cuda.cu`, `mmvq.cu` (no q2_kv vector product: cuBLAS takes it, as for q3_0), `fattn-common.cuh`, `fattn.cu`, `fattn-vec.cuh`, 19 new `template-instances/fattn-vec-instance-*q2_kv*.cu` from `generate_cu_files.py`), `ggml/cmake/common.cmake` and `ggml/CMakeLists.txt` (the default flash attention pairs gain `q2_kv-q2_kv` and `q4_0-q2_kv`; other pairs with q2_kv, such as q8_0-q2_kv or q3_0-q2_kv, are built with `GGML_CUDA_FA_QUANTS=all` or by naming them, and otherwise fall back to f16 with a warning, as for q3_0), `common/arg.cpp` (`kv_cache_types`), `tests/test-quantize-fns.cpp` (q2_kv's error limits). The card-sharing backend needs nothing: types go over the wire by number, so an older far side refuses a q2_kv tensor, as with q3_0. `llama-bench` keeps its own short list of cache types and takes no q2_kv (nor q3_0).
- **Card backends:** CUDA has the writing, the reading and the flash attention vector kernel. Vulkan and Metal have no q2_kv kernels; as for q3_0, that attention would fall to the processor (unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test).
- **Why:** the owner asked for a 2-bit chat memory under Progressive Memory: the old words at 2.5 bits a value, the newest 8K at q8_0. On Qwen 3.6 35B at 256K that is about 0.78 GB of main cache against 1.1 GB for q3_0 and 1.4 GB for q4_0, plus 0.17 GB for the q8_0 ring.
- **What to expect:** rougher than q3_0 on its own. On normal (rotated-like) made-up data the round trip error is 0.34 of the signal (q3_0 0.17, q4_0 0.09; with d fixed at the largest / 1.5 it was 0.45); in a one-word flash attention over 1,024 random K/V of head size 128, the result is off by 0.47 of its size (0.68 with the fixed scale), against 0.24 for q3_0 and 0.12 for q4_0. ## Memory paths on the card (map for the borrowing rebuild)

Written Nov 22 before the borrowing work (scope: rebuild Avalon's ideas inside Brain Engine, credit Avalon, MIT). Line numbers are b12359 with patches 0001 to 0047 applied; they drift, the names don't.

**Who owns card memory, in the order it's made**
1. **Weights** (`llama-model.cpp`, load). With experts in RAM (`--n-cpu-moe` / `-ot exps=CPU`), only the rest of the brain sits on the card.
2. **Chat memory (KV)** (`llama-kv-cache.cpp`, constructor): one buffer per card, sized for the whole context up front. 0036 can put part of it in RAM, 0039/0045 add the ring.
3. **The expert cache** (`llama-moe-cache.cpp`, `impl::impl`, ~line 640 to 760): per group of same-shaped layers, one **bank** per expert tensor (gate, up, down, or gate_up and down), each a 3D tensor of `n_slots + n_zero + 1` slots. All banks of a card come from **one** buffer (`d.buf`, `llama_memory_bufs::alloc_buf`), in creation order: group 0's banks, then that group's small per-layer tensors (look-ahead guesses, doorbell maps, stand-in scales), then the next group. `n_slots` comes from the size in bytes (`--moe-cache-size`, Bob sends 4 GB today). 0022's `lend()` frees `d.buf` and `restore()` makes it again (~line 2300); that free and malloc, plus the reserve below, is the switch time we're removing.
4. **Working memory (compute buffer)** (`llama_context::sched_reserve`, `llama-context.cpp` ~898): `sched_new` makes a scheduler whose allocator (`ggml_gallocr`, `ggml-alloc.c`) has one buffer per backend. `graph_reserve` builds the worst-case reading graph (`n_ubatch` tokens), then the one-word graph, then the reading graph again; the allocator's buffer is sized to the biggest. 0022 reserves for `min(n_ubatch, lend_ub)` while the cache is in place and reserves again (a new scheduler) at each lend and restore.
5. **CUDA's own pool** (`ggml-cuda.cu`, `ggml_cuda_pool_leg` / `ggml_cuda_pool_vmm`): scratch the kernels take outside the allocator (quantized copies of the input, cuBLAS work). It grows with the step and is never counted by the allocator. On Windows it's the legacy pool (plain `cudaMalloc`). Avalon's crash at a step of 32768 was out of this kind of unplanned room; ours has to be measured, not guessed.

**How a long read gets its experts** (op offload): for a step above 32 tokens the slot maps aren't used (`max_batch`), so each layer's expert weights are copied into the working memory before its expert product. The scheduler asks `llama_context::sched_copy_experts` (~3967), which reads the step's picks, then for each run of picked experts copies the ones the cache holds card-to-card (`llama_moe_cache::copy_experts`) and uploads the rest from RAM. So during a long read the cache only saves uploads (its share of the experts); the step size decides how many times every layer's experts cross PCIe (a 64K prompt at 4096 crosses 16 times, at 16384 four times). That's the whole reason a bigger step reads faster, and why its extra working memory is worth more than cache slots while reading.

**How the allocator places a graph** (`ggml-alloc.c`): every new graph is planned from scratch (`ggml_dyn_tallocr_reset`, then `ggml_gallocr_reserve_n`), from offset 0 up, best-fit. A graph's tensors only ever use the first *its own peak* bytes of the buffer, and the buffer only grows (`new_chunk_size > cur_chunk_size`), never shrinks. The buffer is asked from the buffer type (`ggml_backend_buft_alloc_buffer`) and may be split in up to 16 chunks (`GGML_VBUFFER_MAX_CHUNKS`) if the type has a max size.

**CUDA buffer facts the plan leans on**: a buffer type is "CUDA" if its `get_name` is CUDA's (`ggml_backend_buft_is_cuda`), a buffer if its `free_buffer` is CUDA's; the buffer context is just `{device, dev_ptr}` and frees `dev_ptr` on delete (~945). There's no way yet to make a CUDA buffer over memory someone else owns. The one-word expert product (`mmvq.cu` ~1542) asserts its weights are allocated in one piece, so a bank can't be striped across the card.

**What this means for step 1 (borrowed reading)**
- One card region holds both: the working memory's base is the start of the region, the cache's banks start right after the **own** part (the peak of every graph that runs while writing, measured, not guessed). A reading graph at a bigger step runs over the own part *and* the head of the cache's first bank: those slots are lent while it runs.
- Lent slots are the head slots of the first bank, so for each lent slot index every bank's copy of that slot is out (a slot is only usable whole). Before the first big step, hot experts sitting in those slots move card-to-card into the coldest free slots (fast, on the card), so what's lost is the cold end, as Avalon does. The lent slots are marked empty in the LRU and the slot maps; `copy_experts` skips them.
- Nothing is freed or allocated at a switch, and the scheduler isn't remade: it is reserved once at the biggest step, and the allocator re-plans per graph (cheap, on the processor).
- The step for a read is picked from a table measured at start (`ggml_backend_sched_reserve_size` for 512, 1024, 2048, 4096, 8192, 16384): the biggest whose peak fits the own part plus what the cache may lend. CUDA's pool growth is measured the same way and kept out of the lent room.
- After the read, the lent slots fill again from the normal misses while writing (0031), or behind on a second stream (0023's thread), waiting on an event after the last big graph so nothing writes into memory a graph still reads.
- New in CUDA: `ggml_backend_cuda_buffer_from_ptr(device, ptr, size)`, a CUDA buffer over memory it doesn't own (its free does nothing). New in the cache: a buffer type that copies CUDA's and hands the scheduler that buffer. Both behind `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW=1`; without it, nothing changes.
- First bank only is a limit: if a step needs more than the first bank, the step is smaller. With a 4 GB cache the first bank is ~1.3 GB (gate, up, down) or ~2 GB (gate_up, down); step 3 gives the cache all spare memory, which makes that bank bigger.

### 0048-moe-cache-borrow: reading steps borrow the expert cache's coldest slots
- **Switch (off unless asked for):** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW=1`. `-ub` stays the own step (its working memory is owned); bigger steps up to `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW_UB` (default 16384, at most `-b` and the context) are measured at start and the biggest that fits in the own part plus the cache's first bank becomes the reading step (`n_ubatch`). `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW_SPARE_MIB` (default 512) is kept free on the card for CUDA's own scratch, which grows with the step outside the scheduler. Refused (NOT USED, plain reading at `-ub`) with more than one card, `--moe-cache-cpu-share` above 0, look-ahead, the doorbell, stand-ins, `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND` and `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_READ_LAYERS` (it replaces the last two). - **How:** see "Memory paths on the card" above. The cache makes one buffer per card (the region): the working memory's own part in front, the banks behind (`bw_setup`, again only if the own part grows). The scheduler gets the card's buffer type with its buffers made by `bw_alloc` (`ggml_backend_buft_new_alloc_from`), always a buffer over the region's start (`ggml_backend_cuda_buffer_from_ptr`, or `ggml_backend_rpc_buffer_from_ptr` for the stand-in card), so a bigger graph's working memory runs on into the first bank. ### 0049-moe-cache-tail-shelf: evicted experts kept in the chat memory's unused tail
- **Switch (off unless asked for):** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_TAIL=1`. The chat memory past `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_TAIL_KEEP` cells (default 8192) is lent to the expert cache; the chat takes it back `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_TAIL_STEP` cells (default 8192) at a time. Refused (NOT USED) with more than one card, look-ahead, stand-ins (a processor share and the doorbell are fine since 0058), `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND`, `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_READ_LAYERS`, more than one chat (`-np` above 1), shared or reused cache layers, sliding-window and indexer caches, MLA brains, `LLAMA_KV_SPLIT` and `LLAMA_KV_TIERS`. Works together with 0048. Credit: the idea of lending the KV tail is Avalon's (freriadoc/Avalon, MIT); the code is ours.
- **How:** the banks can't grow into the KV tail (each bank has one stride, and hybrid brains like Qwen3.6 35B keep chat memory in only some layers), so the tail is a second shelf. When a plan evicts an expert, its matrices are copied card to card into a free shelf entry (or over the oldest one); a miss the shelf holds comes back card to card and the entry is freed, so only the rest go up from RAM (`tl_exchange` in `copy`). Large batches copy shelf experts too (`copy_experts`). Entries are made from the far end of each K and V tensor (V only with flash attention). The chat memory's `find_slot` only searches cells below `tail_lim`; when it finds no room, `prepare` (and a state read) raises the limit a step, and the cache forgets the entries there and zeroes the cells, so attention padding never reads expert bytes. The K-shift only turns cells below the limit. A cleared chat memory (`clear(true)`) empties the shelf and lends the tail again.

### 0050-moe-drive-tier-hot-set: cold experts read straight from the drive, and the hot set saved for the next start
- **Switches (off unless asked for):**
  - `LLAMA_MOE_DRIVE=1`, the drive tier. A miss whose pages are not in RAM is read from the brain's file with unbuffered reads, `LLAMA_MOE_DRIVE_THREADS` at once (default 4), into a staging ring of `LLAMA_MOE_DRIVE_MIB` (default 512; pinned when the card has pinned host memory), and uploaded from there. Misses whose pages are in RAM go up from the memory map as before. Needs the memory map: refused with `--load-mode none`. `LLAMA_MOE_DRIVE_ALL=1` (tests) reads every miss from the drive.
  - `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_SAVE=<path>`, the saved hot set. The cache writes a ranking file (`bob-moe-ranking 2`, stamped with the brain's fingerprint) holding each expert's CRF decayed to now. It writes every `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_SAVE_EVERY` cache updates (default 4096) and when the cache closes, through `<path>.tmp`. Given as `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_RANKING` at the next start, the cache begins with last session's busiest experts (0016's ranked fill).
- **Why:** some brains are bigger than RAM: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at Q4_K_XL is 111 GB, and npc has 32 GB. There, a miss read through the memory map faults in one page at a time while the card waits. Big unbuffered reads, several at once, go at the drive's speed, and they don't push warm experts out of the system's file cache. The disk tier idea is Strata's and colibrì's; the code is ours.
- **How:** `llama_model::expert_file_of` holds the file path and offset of each `_exps` tensor. It is filled at load only when asked for and the brain is mapped. In `copy()`, each run of misses is checked on its first and last page: `mincore` on Linux and Mac, `QueryWorkingSetEx` on Windows. Runs not in RAM become jobs. Worker threads read them unbuffered, each with its own `llama_file` per brain file, and the jobs are uploaded in order. When the ring is full, it waits for the card's uploads and starts over.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-model.{h,cpp}`, `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:**
  - `llama_file`'s direct I/O and `read_raw`

  - the ranking file format (0016)
- **Tests:** `engine/tests/moe-drive.py`, the wide tiny brain on the stand-in card. 9 of 9 pass (Nov 22):
  - it turns on and reads misses from the drive (5,097 runs), with the words of a plain run
  - a 1 MiB ring waits 1,283 times and gives the same words
  - without `_ALL`, the misses are found in RAM and go up from the map
  - NOT USED with `--load-mode none`
  - the hot set is saved, stamped with the brain, and the next start fills from it with the same words
  - moe-tail.py and moe-learn.py still pass
  - Unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test: Windows (the `QueryWorkingSetEx` check and unbuffered reads), a real card, Flash-Next, and the speed against plain mmap.

### 0051-moe-borrow-every-bank: borrowed reading lends from every bank, not just the first
- **Why:** Gate 1 on npc (BE29, Nov 22) never read past `-ub 4096`. Step 8192 needed 1,726 MiB borrowed, but 0048 could lend only the first bank's slots, 1,126 MiB of a 4 GB cache.

### 0052-moe-borrow-whole-blocks: the striped slot is a whole number of blocks of every bank's type
- **Why:** BE31 on npc wrote "3333…" on every borrow row (fixed rows normal). The CUDA expert products (mmvq, mmq, mmf) take the expert stride in blocks of the bank's own type (`s02 = nb[2]/type_size`). 0051's slot is the sum of the matrix sizes; with Q4_K gates (144-byte blocks) and Q6_K or Q8_0 downs it isn't a whole number of either, so the stride was rounded down and every expert but the first was read from the wrong place. The processor and the stand-in card take the stride in bytes, which is why the tests passed.
- **How:** `bw_slot_bytes()` pads the slot up to the least common multiple of 256 and every bank's block size, and `bw_setup` makes room for that padding after the group's last bank. If padding would waste more than an eighth of a slot, the first bank alone is lent (0048's layout) with a warning. `bw_stripe` asserts the stride is whole blocks for every bank. The region line says "padded to whole blocks" when it pads.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** any new CUDA expert product that divides `nb[2]` by the type size.
- **Tests:** moe-borrow.py 11 of 11 (Nov 22): a new brain with Q4_K gates and Q8_0 downs lends every bank, padded, with the same words as a plain run at that step. moe-tail.py and moe-drive.py pass. On npc's card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0053-moe-drive-standins: stand-ins only for experts on the drive alone
- **Why:** Flash-Next Q4_K_XL on npc (BE29, 4 GB cache, 73 GB of experts on the host) hit 52-54% while writing and uploaded about 1.2 TB a job. A miss whose pages have left RAM makes the card wait on the drive. The idea: use the next best expert that's already in RAM instead of reading from the drive.
- **How:** `LLAMA_MOE_STANDIN=drive` is a new way for 0043's stand-ins. While writing (one-word batches), a missed pick is stood in for only if `sd_in_ram` (0050's `dr_resident` on each matrix's first and last page) says it is not in RAM. The stand-in is the best-scored unpicked expert that is on the card or in RAM; one in RAM is copied up by the plan as usual. The top pick is never stood in for; up to `n_expert_used - 1` a layer a word (`LLAMA_MOE_STANDIN_MAX` caps it). The missed expert is queued (at most 512, no repeats) for one background thread that `madvise(WILLNEED)`s and touches its pages, so the system reads it into RAM for later words. Misses in RAM are copied as before, and long reads never stand in. Unlike the other ways it works next to borrowed reading (0048), which only lends before long reads. The log line counts misses on the drive alone, stand-ins (and how many came from RAM), and background reads done. `LLAMA_MOE_STANDIN_TEST_NO_RAM=1` (tests) treats every expert as not in RAM.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** 0043's stand-in hooks in `llama-graph.cpp`.
- **Tests:** moe-drive-standin.py 10 of 10 (Nov 22): with the brain in RAM nothing is stood in and the words match a plain run. ### 0057-moe-pinned-upload: long reads' uploads from RAM through a pinned ring
- **Why:** on a long read, the experts the cache doesn't hold are uploaded from the brain's memory map, which is plain (pageable) memory. CUDA then stages each copy through its own small pinned buffer and returns only once the bytes are staged, so the upload runs well under PCIe speed and page faults land on the card's stream. Avalon avoids this on Linux by registering the brain's memory, which Windows refuses for a read-only file map (Avalon #47). Step 1 of the BE34 streaming design (another thread, Nov 22).
- **How:** `LLAMA_MOE_PINNED_UPLOAD=1` (off unless set). The copy callback's uploads (`sched_copy_experts`) go through two slots of pinned memory (`LLAMA_MOE_PINNED_UPLOAD_MIB`, default 64 each, made with `cudaMallocHost` even when `GGML_CUDA_NO_PINNED` is set). Copy threads (`LLAMA_MOE_PINNED_UPLOAD_THREADS`, default 4, the calling thread is one) fill a slot from the map while the card takes the other; an event per slot says when it is free again. Copies under 256 KiB go direct as before. The same bytes reach the same place, so the words don't change. A line per long read gives the MiB, pieces, copy time and time waiting for a slot. Without pinned memory it says `!!! pinned upload NOT USED` and runs as before.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp`, `src/llama-context.h`, `ggml/src/ggml-cuda/ggml-cuda.cu` (two proc-address functions for pinned memory), `ggml/src/ggml-rpc/ggml-rpc.cpp` (a test-only `offload_op`, on only with `GGML_RPC_TEST_OFFLOAD=1`, so the stand-in card takes big expert products from RAM as a real card does).

### 0058-moe-borrow-next-to-doorbell: borrowed reading and the shelf next to a processor share, the doorbell and the learning cache
- **Why:** borrowed reading (0048) and the shelf (0049) refused any processor share, so a brain too big for the card could have Avalon's way of reading or the doorbell (0042) and the learning cache (0031) for writing, not both. For Flash-Next on a 16 GB card, about half the experts miss while writing, which is what the doorbell is for.
- **How:** both refusals are lifted for a processor share, the doorbell and the learning cache (and 0032's processor reading). Borrowing: the zero slots sit after the cached slots and a read lends at most the cached slots, so they stay zero. After borrowing moves or drops experts, and after rewarm (0056) puts them back, the doorbell's mirrors are made again before the next word; until the cache is full again the doorbell waits and the old path fills it, as 0042 already does at load. The shelf: misses left to the processor never come in, so they never touch it; the ones that come in go through it as before, and with the doorbell, between words: the expert a slot really holds goes onto the shelf before the slot is filled, and a fill the shelf holds comes from it on the card. Look-ahead (0041) and stand-ins other than 0053's drive mode are still refused.
- **Lossless:** yes. The same bytes reach the same slots. At share 1 with learning off, the words are a plain run's; below share 1 the doorbell's own drift (0042) is unchanged.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** nothing outside the Brain Engine code.
- **Tests:** moe-borrow-doorbell.py 12 of 12 (Nov 22, cloud, stand-in card, the processor's own ring): at share 1 the same words as a plain run at the borrowed step, with and without the doorbell; at share 0.5 the same words without the doorbell, and with it, it rings and answers (below share 1 the doorbell's words follow what the cache holds, so its drift gate applies); with the learning cache too, it rings and answers; look-ahead still refused. moe-tail.py 10 of 10: at share 0.5 the shelf is used with a plain run's words; with the doorbell, 3,589 experts onto the shelf and 2,728 back from it between words, with the words of the doorbell without the shelf. moe-borrow.py (its share check now expects borrowing on) and moe-doorbell.py pass. On a real card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0059-moe-stream-ahead: a long read sends a layer's other expert weights ahead on a second stream
- **Why:** on a long read, each expert weight in RAM (gate, up, down) waited for its own upload before it computed, so the card's copies and its work took turns. The three weights of a layer share the same picks, so once the first one's picks are read, the others' missing experts are known exactly. Step 2 of the BE34 streaming design; idea from Avalon (freriadoc/Avalon, MIT), code ours.
- **How:** `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_AHEAD=1` (off unless set). After the first expert weight of a layer is handled in the copy callback, the picked experts of the layer's other weights that the cache (or the shelf) doesn't hold go up on a second stream of the card into a landing zone (`LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_AHEAD_MIB`, default 256 MiB of card memory; experts that don't fit go up as before). With pinned memory and events (a real card) a thread queues those copies through its own pinned ring (0057's), so the processor's copying overlaps the card's work. When the scheduler asks for the next weight, the card's stream waits for the landing zone on an event (never a processor flag) and copies the experts card to card. The landing zone is used again from the next layer's picks on (the card's stream is idle then). A line per long read gives the experts sent ahead and used, the layers, how often the zone was full, and the wait. Refused next to 0019 (which it replaces). `llama_moe_cache::holds` says whether the cache would find an expert.
- **Lossless:** yes. The same bytes reach the same place.
- **Memory:** the landing zone is extra card memory, not counted by the fit: take it off `--moe-cache-mib` (or the fit target). Two more 64 MiB pinned slots in RAM for its ring.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp`, `src/llama-context.h`, `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`, `src/llama-moe-cache.h`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** the copy callback (`sched_copy_experts`), `llama_layer`'s expert weight names.
- **Tests:** moe-stream-ahead.py 9 of 9 (Nov 22, cloud, stand-in card, its steps queued in line): 1,530 experts sent ahead and all used over 36 layers, the same words as a plain run, without the cache, next to the pinned ring, with a 1 MiB zone (full 72 times), next to borrowed reading; NOT USED next to 0019. moe-pinned-upload.py still passes. The thread that queues the copies and the overlap itself need a real card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0060-moe-ram-cache: Bob's own RAM cache for experts
- **Why:** the biggest gap to Avalon on Flash-Next (the Avalon study, Nov 22): Avalon keeps an owned block of RAM (about 23 GB on 32 GB) for experts, hottest first, least recently used out, with drive reads landing in it. Brain Engine leaned on the system's file cache: the drive tier (0050) reads past it and forgets each expert once uploaded, and the map's pages come and go as Windows likes, so npc thrashed at 1.3-1.75 words/s.
- **How:** `LLAMA_MOE_RAM_CACHE_MIB=N` (off unless set; refused, NOT USED, without `LLAMA_MOE_DRIVE=1`). The block is made in pieces of `LLAMA_MOE_RAM_CACHE_CHUNK_MIB` (default 256) from the card's host buffer type (pinned), and in plain memory when the system refuses more pinned memory or `LLAMA_MOE_RAM_CACHE_PLAIN=1`. Entries hold one expert of one matrix and are grouped by size (gate, up and down may be different types); each size gets its share by how much of the brain's experts it makes up. In `copy`'s upload loop each miss goes through `rc_take`: a RAM hit goes up from its entry; a miss whose pages are in RAM is copied in from the map; any other is read by the drive tier's workers straight into an entry (`dr_upload` reads such jobs first, then the ring's). An entry uploaded in the current copy call is never replaced in it (the next call starts after the card is done, `rc_gen`); when every entry is in use the miss passes by to the ring as before. A start-fill thread reads the ranking's (`LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_RANKING`, or the saved hot set) busiest experts the card doesn't hold into empty entries only; `LLAMA_MOE_RAM_CACHE_PREFILL=0` turns it off. The totals line says hits, drive reads into it, map copies, pass-bys and start-fill entries. The same bytes go up, so the words don't change.
- **For other uploaders:** `llama_moe_cache::ram_lookup(w, e)` gives the RAM cache's copy of an expert (or nullptr), marked as used, valid until the next copy call (so 0059's staging can upload from it). Main thread only.
- **Not yet through it:** the large-batch path (`copy_experts`), look-ahead (0041), the doorbell's fills (0042), rewarm (0056) and 0057/0059's long-read uploads still read the map. Stand-ins on the drive (0053) don't count a RAM-cache entry as in RAM yet.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** nothing outside the Brain Engine code.
- **Tests:** moe-ram-cache.py 9 of 9 (Nov 22, cloud, stand-in card, so plain memory): 381 drive reads into it then 4,740 RAM hits with a plain run's words; a 1 MiB block passes misses by with the same words; plain memory in 1 MiB pieces, evicting, same words; map copies without _ALL; a start fill of 255 matrices from the saved hot set, same words; NOT USED without the drive tier. moe-drive.py and moe-drive-standin.py still pass. On npc: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0063-moe-borrow-last-row: nothing is filled into lent slots while a read runs
- **Why:** a wrong-words bug in borrowed reading (0048) with rewarm (0056), found Nov 23 while building 0065. A read's last step works out only its last token in the last layer (one row), so the MoE cache took that layer as a small batch: rewarm, and the misses of that one row, were written into the slots the read had lent, while that same graph's working memory still lived there. On the stand-in card at -ub 16 (own part 0.77 MiB, reading step 1,024) the first word's odds were another brain's (top word at -0.23 instead of -0.85); plain runs at steps 16, 512 and 1,024 agree to 4 decimals, so it is not rounding. With a bigger own part the writes happened to land on dead space, which is why moe-borrow.py (own step 64) never saw it. Affects Brain Engine 33 to 35 with `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW=1`; rewarm off (`LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_REWARM=0`) avoids the rewarm half only.
- **How:** no switch (a fix, lossless). Before each graph, `bw_before_graph` notes its working memory (`bw_cur_peak`) and the slots it runs over (`lru.lent_below`); `victim()` skips those slots, and rewarm waits while the graph's working memory is past the owned part. The next writing graph (inside the owned part) fills them as before.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** that the last layer still works out only the output rows, and that `build_moe_ffn` still asks the cache per op (`selected_experts->ne[1]`).
- **Tests:** moe-borrow-last-row.py 3 of 3 (Nov 23, cloud, stand-in card): borrowed reading at -ub 16 gives the first word's odds and the words of a plain run at its reading step, with rewarm on and off, and rewarm still runs before writing. The same run before the fix gave the wrong odds (-0.23 for the top word). On a real card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0064-moe-step-fit-equal-parts: the reading step fitted to a line, and reads cut into equal parts
- **Why:** borrowed reading (0048) tried steps by doubling (1024, 2048, 4096...), so a card with room for 6,000 read at 4,096. And a read was cut into full steps and a small rest: BE29's 128K read ended in an 11-token piece, which went another way through the cache (the 18% finding). Avalon fits a line and cuts each prompt into equal parts. Ideas from Avalon (freriadoc/Avalon, MIT), code ours.
- **How:** two switches, both off unless set. `LLAMA_MOE_STEP_FIT=1` (with borrowed reading): the working memory measured at the steps tried is fitted to a line (need = a + b x tokens); the biggest step on a 256-token grid the line says fits what the cache lends is measured for real, a grid step lower each time it doesn't fit (up to 8 times); the card's scratch spare then steps it down a grid step at a time instead of halving. Avalon keeps a margin of 256 MiB + 10% on its guess; here the guess is measured, so it has none. `LLAMA_EQUAL_PARTS=1`: a batch longer than the step is cut into k = ceil(n / step) parts of ceil(n / k) tokens, rounded up to 256 and never more than the step (so the working memory measured for the step holds). Logs `fitted step: need = ...` once and `equal parts: N tokens in K parts of up to P (step S)` per batch.
- **Words:** a different step reads with different sums, so both change the words against today's step, as any step change does (the 18% finding). Against a plain run at the step they pick, the words are the same (the test checks both). So: same words at the same step, not the same words as today; Calibrate compares at the same text sha.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `sched_reserve`'s borrowed-step search and `decode`'s `init_batch` call.
- **Tests:** moe-step-fit.py 6 of 6 (Nov 23, cloud, stand-in card): the fitted step 1,536 where doubling picked 1,024, with a plain step-1,536 run's words; equal parts cut 1,536 tokens at step 1,024 into 2 of 768, with a plain step-768 run's words; both off unless set. On a real card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0065-moe-borrow-write: borrowed writing buffers
- **Why:** with borrowed reading (0048) the card still owned the working memory of the own step (`-ub`) all the time, though writing needs far less. Avalon borrows its compute buffers from the expert cache and owns only what decoding needs. Idea from Avalon (freriadoc/Avalon, MIT), code ours.
- **How:** `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW_WRITE=1` (off unless set, with `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_BORROW=1`). `sched_reserve` measures writing (a graph of up to 16 tokens, so drafts fit; `=N` sets N) and the card owns only that; the own step borrows the cache's coldest slots like any bigger step. A read no bigger than the own step lends only what its graph needs (`borrow_set_small`), so a tool's answer of a few hundred tokens doesn't empty a whole reading step's slots; bigger reads lend the reading step at once, as before. If the cache can't lend the own step, it says `!!! borrowed writing NOT USED` and the card owns it as before. Logs `borrowed writing: writing (up to N tokens) owns X MiB, the own step S borrows its Y MiB: Z MiB of the card freed for the cache`.
- **Words:** the same steps and graphs, so the same words as borrowed reading alone (lossless; the test checks it).
- **Where the freed memory goes:** it stays free on the card. `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_FILL=1` (0061) or a bigger `--moe-cache-mib` turns it into cache; with the fill, the owned part must fit in its spare, which is easier now that it is only writing's.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp` (`sched_reserve`), `src/llama-moe-cache.{h,cpp}`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** as 0048.
- **Tests:** moe-borrow-write.py 4 of 4 (Nov 23, cloud, stand-in card): own part 4.01 MiB to 0.77 MiB (3.24 MiB freed), the words of borrowed reading alone over a short read, two long reads and writing, the short read lent 3 slots where the long ones lent 21, off unless set. Needs 0063 (without it the small own part shows the last-row bug). On a real card: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.

### 0067-moe-stream-next: the next layer streamed ahead, and copies from the RAM cache
- **Why:** the Avalon study's biggest gap (Nov 22): on a long read Avalon streams only the misses, on a second stream, behind the work of the layer before. Stream ahead (0059) only sent a layer's other weights after its first one started, so each layer's first weight still went up with the card's stream waiting; and the long-read uploads and 0059's staging still read the brain file's map even when the RAM cache (0060) held the expert. Split with the speed thread: this is the streaming half; reading cached experts in place is 0068. Idea from Avalon (freriadoc/Avalon, MIT), code ours.
- **How:** `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_NEXT=1` (off unless set, with `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_AHEAD=1`; NOT USED with a warning when no two layers in a row keep expert weights in RAM or there's no room). When a layer of a long read picks at least `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_NEXT_PCT` percent of its experts (default 50), the next layer's first expert weight goes up on the second stream into its own zone of `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_NEXT_MIB` (default 256) while this layer computes: every expert of it the copy cache doesn't hold, until the zone is full. When that layer starts, what was sent is copied card to card from the zone (after an event on a real card); the rest goes up as before. The second stream waits for the card's stream to have read the zone before filling it again. With no switch: any expert the RAM cache (0060) holds comes from its copy, both in 0059/0067's staging and in the long-read uploads (`ram_lookup`); copies on the second stream are done before the MoE cache's next `copy()` call may replace them (`llama_moe_cache::copies`).
- **Words:** the same bytes reach the same place, so the same words (lossless); a wrong guess only sends an expert up early for nothing.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.cpp` (`llama_stream_ahead`, `sched_copy_experts`), `src/llama-moe-cache.{h,cpp}` (`copies`).

### 0068-cuda-wddm-budget: on Windows, plan card memory by WDDM's budget
- **Why:** on Windows, `cudaMemGetInfo` doesn't count what WDDM has promised other programs (a game, the desktop), and going over doesn't fail: WDDM quietly moves memory to shared system memory over PCIe and the brain gets very slow. ### 0069-moe-read-in-place: long reads use the cached experts where they sit
- **Why:** a batch of more than 32 tokens bypassed the cache. The scheduler copied every used expert of a layer into a working copy of the weight before each step: the cached ones card to card, the rest from RAM. So the step's working memory held a copy of an expert weight, and every step copied the hits again. Avalon reads cached experts in place (see `avalon-how-it-shares-card-memory.md`, item 1). Idea from Avalon (freriadoc/Avalon, MIT), code ours.
- **How:** on unless `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_READ_IN_PLACE=0`. `get_slot_map` gives long reads without a CPU part the slot map, so they read the banks through `mul_mat_id` like writing does. At the first layer of a long read, `ip_begin` takes a zone out of each group's coldest slots, as big as the most misses any layer of the group can have (so every layer always fits; the LRU doesn't change during a read). In each layer's copy callback, `ip_copy` reads the picks, maps hits to their slots and misses to zone slots in expert order, uploads the misses there through `upload_fills` (the old upload loop, now shared: RAM cache, drive tier and RAM floor as before) and sends that map. The first other lookup after the read calls `ip_end`, which puts the experts the zone took out back into it, hottest first, before the first written word. Off, with a NOT USED line, next to `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_LEND`, `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_READ_LAYERS`, `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_AHEAD` and `LLAMA_MOE_STREAM_NEXT` (they work on the layer copies), and while borrowed reading (0048) is on. A group with fewer slots than a layer has experts reads the stock way. One log line at the end gives reads, experts read in place, uploads, the biggest zone and the experts put back.
- **Lossless:** yes. The same experts are multiplied, read from other places on the card.
- **Memory:** the step's working memory no longer holds a copy of an expert weight (on the stand-in, 22.0 to 18.0 MiB at `-ub 512` and 80.1 to 72.1 MiB at `-ub 2048`, about one weight of one layer). During a long read the zone takes up to one layer's experts of the coldest cache slots; they come back before writing. With 0061 the freed working memory goes to the cache.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-moe-cache.cpp`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `build_moe_cache_slots` (the lookup is still made for big batches), CUDA's `mul_mat_id` with many slots on big batches.
- **Tests:** moe-read-in-place.py 12 of 12 (Nov 22, cloud, stand-in card): on by default, the same words as =0 and as without the cache over two long prompts with writing between, with the RAM cache and drive tier, at `-ub 2048`; working memory smaller; NOT USED next to stream ahead; a cache smaller than a layer reads as before. ### 0071-moe-read-ahead: read ahead inside the in-place long read
- **Why:** 0069 reads the cached experts in place, but each layer still waits for its own misses to go up before it can start. Stream ahead (0059, 0067) hid that wait behind the layer before, but it works on the layer copies 0069 replaced, so the two could not run together. A long read's layer picks nearly every expert, so the next layer's misses are known before its router runs: every expert the cache doesn't hold. At the end of a layer's lookup, the next layer's misses are sent into its half on a second stream of the card (`ggml_backend_dev_init` on the same device), from the RAM cache (0060) when it holds them, else from RAM, in expert order. On CUDA a thread queues those copies so the processor's copying overlaps the card's work; on the stand-in card they run in line (`LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_READ_AHEAD_THREAD=1` forces the thread). The next layer's lookup waits for them, maps its picks to where they landed, and uploads only what wasn't sent (its first layer, or a group that had no room for two halves). `ip_end` waits before putting the zone's experts back. NOT USED, with a line, without in-place reads or next to the drive tier (it would read the brain file around the drive tier). One log line at the end: layers and experts sent ahead, MiB, parts from the RAM cache, experts used, groups with one zone, and time waited.
- **Words:** no change. The same bytes sit in the slots the layer reads; only which slots differs.
- **Memory:** the zone is up to twice 0069's during a long read, from the coldest cache slots, and comes back before writing. ### 0072-moe-cache-fill-leaves-work-room: a filled cache leaves room for the working memory
- **Why:** 0061 sizes the filled cache before the working memory (the compute buffers) exists, keeping only a 512 MiB spare. A big reading step's buffer is larger than that. On the test PC (BE36, 35B, 32K job, Windows) the filled cache came to 5,503 MiB and the card spilled 1,030 MB into shared memory. Prompt reading fell from 2,829 to 1,223 t/s, and the first word came at 10.1 s instead of 4.4 s, even though writing was 5% faster.
- **How:** only when 0061 grew the cache, in the real load. The first `sched_reserve` measures the working memory of the biggest step and of writing on each card (`graph_reserve` with sizes, nothing allocated), reads each card's free memory with the cache already in, and adds up how far short of working memory plus the spare each card is. If any card is short, the cache is made again that much smaller (never under `--moe-cache-mib`) and the scheduler is made new. One log line either way. With borrowed reading or the shelf, the working memory comes from the cache, so it doesn't check. `LLAMA_MOE_CACHE_FILL_TEST_FREE_MIB` overrides the free figure for the engine test only, because the stand-in card's free memory doesn't move.
- **Words:** no change; only the cache size changes.
- **Upstream files:** `src/llama-context.{h,cpp}`.
- **Re-check on rebase:** `sched_reserve`'s order (measure before the real reserve), and `graph_reserve`'s sizes path.
- **Tests:** moe-cache-fill.py 7 of 7 (Nov 23, cloud, stand-in card). The filled cache measures 2 MiB of working memory and fits. ### 0073-cuda-doorbell-fuses-like-share1: the doorbell's experts fuse like share 1's
- **Why:** the doorbell (0042) wrote different words from share 1 from about word 64 (Calibrate, BE36). The two give the same words with `GGML_CUDA_DISABLE_FUSION=1` on both (Nov 23), so the difference comes from CUDA's kernel fusion, not a race or the processor's part. With fusion off, the doorbell is still 16% faster (93.8 vs 80.9 t/s).
- **How:** CUDA fuses a layer's routed experts and its shared expert into one kernel (`ggml_cuda_match_shared_expert`, used by both `graph_optimize` and `graph_compute`) only when both are in the same split. Without the doorbell, the processor's part splits the graph between them, so they never fuse. With the doorbell, the layer is one split, so they did, and the fused kernel adds up in a different order. Now an expert product whose ids come from the doorbell's ring (`GGML_OP_MOE_DOORBELL`, through views) doesn't take that fusion, so it uses the same kernels as share 1. `GGML_CUDA_DOORBELL_SHARED_FUSION=1` fuses them anyway. For finding the next such case: `GGML_CUDA_FUSION_OFF=<list>` turns off single fusion families (`shared`, `glu`, `topk`, `reduce`; `glu` also stops `shared`).
- **Words:** the doorbell should now match share 1. That's the hypothesis; it's unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test on the test PC. If it still differs, the next step is the bisect with `GGML_CUDA_FUSION_OFF`.

- **Re-check on rebase:** the fusion matchers' names and that `graph_optimize` and `try_fuse` still share them.
- **Tests:** CUDA build (Nov 23, cloud, compiles; no card here). On the test PC: unproven in our lab, as we have yet to test.
